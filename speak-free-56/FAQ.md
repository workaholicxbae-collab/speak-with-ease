# FAQ

The questions people actually ask, answered without softening.

---

## "I missed three days. Should I start again?"

No. Restarting is the most common way this program dies.

Pick up at the day you stopped. Do not do three days in one session to catch up — you will exhaust yourself and quit properly this time.

The calendar does not matter. The sequence does. Day 12 works after a five-day gap exactly as well as it works on the twelfth consecutive day.

**If you missed more than two weeks:** go back three days from where you stopped and run those again. Not as punishment — the momentum genuinely fades, and three days rebuilds it.

---

## "My accent is the problem."

It is not, and this program will not change it.

Accent affects how you sound. Fluency affects whether you can speak at all. You are here because you freeze, not because you pronounce "th" a particular way — and no listener has ever stopped a conversation because of an accent they could still understand.

The people you think of as confident English speakers include millions with strong accents. What they have is *momentum*, and momentum is trainable. Accent mostly is not, past a certain age, and chasing it is the most efficient way to spend a year and gain nothing.

**Nothing in this program is scored on accent.** Check the rubric. It is not one of the five dimensions, on any day.

---

## "I have nobody to speak English with."

Then use the solo fallback, which exists on every single day. Not most days. Every day.

The program was built assuming you are alone, and the partner options are upgrades, not requirements. You can complete all fifty-six days without speaking to another human being.

That said, four days ask you to reach a real person — Days 5, 19, 27 and 55. For those:

- A voice note counts.
- A call-centre agent counts.
- A shopkeeper counts.
- An AI voice mode counts, and is genuinely useful.

If you use the AI option every time, be honest with yourself that you chose the comfortable path. That honesty is more useful than the guilt.

---

## "I hate my recorded voice."

Everyone does, and there is a physical reason. When you speak, you hear yourself through the bones of your skull, which adds depth. A recording removes that. What you hear is what everyone else has always heard — you are simply not used to it.

You do not need to like it. You need to stop flinching from it, and that happens through exposure, not through effort.

By Day 20 you will notice you no longer brace before pressing play.

---

## "I understand everything but I cannot speak. Why?"

Because they are different skills, and you have practised only one.

Reading and listening are *recognition* — the words are given to you and your brain matches them. Speaking is *production* — you generate from nothing, in real time, with no menu to choose from.

You have done thousands of hours of recognition and possibly ten hours of production. The imbalance is not a defect. It is a training history, and this program corrects it.

---

## "Should I study grammar alongside this?"

Not in Weeks 1–4. Grammar study strengthens the internal checking habit, which is the exact thing making you freeze.

From Week 5, if you want to, keep it to fifteen minutes and keep it separate — different time of day, different notebook. Never during a speaking session.

---

## "How long until I see a difference?"

You will *hear* one on Day 7. You will *feel* one somewhere in Week 3, usually mid-sentence, when you realise you have been talking for a while without checking.

Other people typically notice around Week 5.

The gap between hearing it and feeling it is normal and frustrating. This is why the Day 1 recording exists — on the days you feel nothing has changed, the file disagrees with you, and the file is right.

---

## "I froze completely and could not do the day."

Then do the same day tomorrow with an `easy` prompt from [resources/prompt-bank.md](resources/prompt-bank.md).

Freezing is information, not failure. It tells you the difficulty dial was set too high. Turn it down and run the same day again — the tags exist precisely for this.

Do not skip forward. Days build on each other, and Week 2 assumes sixty unbroken seconds is available to you.

---

## "Can I do two days in one session?"

No.

The gap between sessions is where the work consolidates. Two days back to back gives you two mediocre sessions instead of one good one, and it is usually a sign you are trying to buy back guilt from a missed day.

You cannot. Just do today's day.

---

## "What if I finish all 56 days and still feel nervous?"

You will still feel some nerves. That is not a failure of the program — nerves before speaking are permanent, including for people who speak professionally for a living.

What changes is what happens *next*. Before: nerves, then freeze. After: nerves, then speak anyway.

That second pattern is the entire goal. Day 56 asks you to write a letter to yourself and build a ninety-day maintenance plan, because Day 56 is not the end of the work — it is the point where the work stops needing a program.

---

## "Is 20 minutes really enough?"

Twenty minutes of speaking out loud, daily, is more production than most learners do in a month.

The limit is deliberate. Ninety-minute sessions get abandoned by day nine — you have probably already proved this to yourself with an app. Twenty-two minutes survives a bad week, and surviving bad weeks is what finishes programs.
