# Week 3 — Everyday Conversation

> **Conversation is a rally, not a performance. Your job is to return the ball.**

---

## The lie you have been believing

*"When it's my turn, I have to say something good."*

This is why conversation exhausts you. You treat every turn as a small exam where a complete, correct, interesting answer is due — and while you are building it, the moment passes.

Nobody else is doing this. The people you envy are not producing excellent sentences. They are returning the ball: a short answer, a reaction, a question back. Then it is not their turn any more.

Your standard is far too high, and it is what makes you slow.

---

## What this week does to you

You stop practising monologues and start practising *exchanges*.

The unit of work changes. It is no longer "speak for sixty seconds" — it is "answer, react, ask back", which is a much smaller thing to produce and a much more useful one.

By Day 19 you will have three real minutes with a real person. By Day 21 you will hold an unscripted conversation for three minutes and hear it back.

## Weekly challenge

**Ask a stranger one question in English and listen to the whole answer.**

A shopkeeper, a colleague you do not know, a person at a counter. "Sorry, do you know if…" is enough.

The challenge is not the question. It is staying present for the entire answer instead of spending it planning your next sentence. Most people never hear the answers they ask for.

---

## Exit criteria

By the end of Day 21 you should be able to do all four:

- [ ] **Open a conversation** with a stranger without rehearsing the first line.
- [ ] **Ask two follow-up questions** that come from what they said, not from a script.
- [ ] **React within one second** — "really?", "that's awful", "no way" — instead of pausing.
- [ ] **Hold three minutes** of unscripted back-and-forth without switching languages.

The third one matters more than it looks. The one-second reaction is what makes you feel present to the other person, and it costs almost no English.

---

## What most people get wrong this week

**They prepare their answers.** Then the conversation goes somewhere else and the preparation is useless — worse, it becomes something to grieve mid-sentence.

**They treat questions as an obligation, not a tool.** A question hands the work back to the other person and buys you fifteen seconds. It is the most underused move available to you.

**They skip Day 19** because it involves a real human. Day 19 is the point of the week. The roleplay days are rehearsal for it.
