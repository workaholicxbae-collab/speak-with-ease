# Week 1 — Break the Silence

> **Your mouth moves before your brain approves. Output beats accuracy.**

---

## The lie you have been believing

*"I need to know more before I can speak."*

You have believed this for years, and it is the reason you are still stuck. You have been preparing to speak since school. You have more English inside you than most people who speak it confidently in your office.

The gap is not knowledge. It is that you have built a habit of checking the sentence before releasing it — and that check takes two seconds, and two seconds is exactly long enough for the conversation to move on without you.

This week does not add anything to what you know. It removes the check.

---

## What this week does to you

You will speak badly on purpose, several times, on record.

That is not a side effect. It is the treatment. The fear you carry is not really of speaking — it is of being heard being imperfect. So we do that immediately, in private, where the stakes are zero, until the alarm stops going off.

By Day 3 you will talk for sixty unbroken seconds. By Day 5 you will have sent English to another human being. Neither will feel comfortable. Both will be done.

## Weekly challenge

**Speak English out loud to one human being who does not live with you.**

Any length. A colleague, a shopkeeper, a call-centre agent, a stranger who asks the time. Thirty seconds counts.

The only rule: it must be a person who could, in principle, judge you. That is the entire difficulty, and the entire value.

If you genuinely cannot reach one this week, use an AI voice mode — but be honest with yourself about which one you chose and why.

---

## Exit criteria

By Sunday night you should be able to do all four of these, measurably:

- [ ] **Speak for 60 seconds without stopping**, on any easy prompt, first take.
- [ ] **Tolerate your own recorded voice** well enough to listen to a full playback without skipping.
- [ ] **Pause in silence** instead of filling the gap with "um" — at least sometimes.
- [ ] **Produce seven recordings**, all saved, none deleted.

If you cannot tick the first one by Day 7, do not move to Week 2. Re-run Day 3 with a different `easy` prompt for two or three days until you can. Week 2 assumes that sixty seconds is available to you.

Everything else on this list will keep improving for the next seven weeks. That one is load-bearing.

---

## What most people get wrong this week

**They re-record Day 1.** Then the whole program loses its measuring stick. Record once. Save it. Walk away.

**They treat Day 2 as a test.** It is not. Shadowing is borrowed fluency — you are wearing someone else's rhythm on loan, and it is supposed to feel like cheating. Cheat.

**They quit somewhere around Day 4** because they have realised the recordings are worse than they hoped. That realisation is the program working. The recording was always that bad. You are simply hearing it for the first time.
