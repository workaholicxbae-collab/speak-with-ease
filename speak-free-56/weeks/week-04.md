# Week 4 — Storytelling & Personality

> **Fluent people are not accurate. They are interesting. Structure buys you time.**

---

## The lie you have been believing

*"I'm boring in English."*

You are not boring. You are compressing.

In your first language you tell a story with detail, timing and a punchline. In English you deliver the summary — *"yes, I went to Delhi, it was fine"* — because summary is safer and shorter and there is less to get wrong.

The result is that people meet a smaller version of you. Not a less fluent one. A smaller one.

---

## What this week does to you

You get a skeleton. Five lines that hold any story upright, so that while you are speaking you always know what comes next.

That is the real function of structure. It is not about elegance — it is that knowing the next beat frees up the attention you have been spending on panic.

By Day 27 you tell a real story to a real person and watch their face while you do it.

## Weekly challenge

**Tell one real story to one real person, unprompted.**

Not when asked. You start it: *"Something strange happened yesterday…"*

Volunteering a story is different from answering a question. It means claiming the floor without being invited — and that claim, far more than vocabulary, is what confident speakers actually do differently.

---

## Exit criteria

By the end of Day 28 you should be able to do all four:

- [ ] **Tell a two-minute story** with a beginning, a turn and an ending, without notes.
- [ ] **Compress the same story to thirty seconds** without losing the point.
- [ ] **Add sensory detail** without slowing down.
- [ ] **Hear a clear difference** between your Day 1 recording and your Day 28 portfolio piece.

The second is the one people underestimate. Compression is harder than expansion, and it is what you need when somebody asks about your weekend and has ninety seconds.

---

## What most people get wrong this week

**They pick a dramatic story.** Then they spend the time managing the plot instead of practising delivery. An ordinary story told well beats a remarkable story told flatly — and the ordinary one is what you will actually need.

**They add detail by slowing down.** Detail should replace vague words, not extend the runtime. "A big dog" becomes "a wet grey dog", not "a big — quite big — dog, quite large actually".

**They skip Day 25** because humour in a second language feels impossible. Day 25 is not about being funny. It is about timing, and timing is a mechanical skill.
