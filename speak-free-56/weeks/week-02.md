# Week 2 — Think in English

> **The delay is not vocabulary. It is translation. Cut the middle step.**

---

## The lie you have been believing

*"I have to get it right in my head first."*

Watch what actually happens when someone asks you a question. You hear it. You understand it instantly. Then you build the answer in your first language, translate it, check the translation, and speak.

That whole process takes two to four seconds. In a real conversation, two seconds is the gap where somebody else starts talking.

The translation step is not making you accurate. It is making you late.

---

## What this week does to you

Every day this week removes the time to translate. Not by telling you to stop — you cannot stop a habit by deciding to. By making the task fast enough that translation becomes physically impossible.

You cannot translate while narrating a video in real time. You cannot translate with thirty seconds per question. The middle step gets squeezed out, and what is left is English going straight from thought to mouth.

It will feel less controlled. That is what direct thinking feels like from the inside.

## Weekly challenge

**One full hour where your inner monologue runs in English.**

Not speaking — thinking. Pick an hour when you are alone and doing something ordinary: cooking, commuting, walking, cleaning.

Every time you notice yourself thinking in your first language, do not restart the hour and do not scold yourself. Just switch back. You will drift dozens of times. Drifting and returning *is* the exercise.

---

## Exit criteria

By the end of Day 14 you should be able to do all four:

- [ ] **Start speaking within two seconds** of hearing a prompt, without a planning pause.
- [ ] **Describe around a word you do not know**, instead of stopping or switching languages.
- [ ] **Narrate something happening in real time** for two minutes without falling behind it.
- [ ] **Hear a measurable difference** between your Day 1 and Day 14 recordings of the same prompt.

The third one is the load-bearing item. Real-time narration is the clearest proof that translation has stopped, because there is no room for it.

If Day 11 defeats you, re-run it with a slower video before moving on.

---

## What most people get wrong this week

**They think "stop translating" is a decision.** It is not. You cannot instruct yourself out of a reflex. You can only starve it of time, which is what every day this week does.

**They panic on Day 12** because thirty seconds feels impossibly short. It is supposed to. Short answers that arrive beat complete answers that do not.

**They skip the weekly challenge** because thinking in English feels like nothing is being produced. It is the most valuable hour of the week — the habit you are building lives in your head, not your mouth.
