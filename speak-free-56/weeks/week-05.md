# Week 5 — Opinions, Agreement, Disagreement

> **Confidence is having a position, not having proof.**

---

## The lie you have been believing

*"I can't say that unless I'm sure."*

So you say "it depends" and hope someone else commits first. You have mistaken this for humility. It reads as absence.

The people who sound confident in meetings are not better informed than you. They have simply noticed that a stated position is a contribution, and a withheld one is nothing at all — and that being wrong out loud costs far less than being silent.

---

## What this week does to you

The work gets harder here, and your scores will probably drop. That is expected and it is not a problem.

For four weeks you have been describing, narrating and telling. Nobody pushed back. From today somebody argues, and you have to hold a position while producing English at the same time.

That is two jobs at once, which is why it feels like a step backwards. It is not. It is the first week where the difficulty matches real life.

## Weekly challenge

**Disagree out loud, once, in English, with a real person.**

Small is fine. A restaurant choice, a scheduling suggestion, an opinion about a film.

The requirement is that you say the disagreeing sentence *before* you say the softening one. Most people bury the disagreement under so much apology it disappears. Say the thing, then soften if you want to.

---

## Exit criteria

By the end of Day 35 you should be able to do all four:

- [ ] **State a position in one sentence** without hedging before you have said it.
- [ ] **Disagree with someone** without apologising first.
- [ ] **Hold a position across five exchanges** without retreating to "well, maybe you're right".
- [ ] **Say what "it depends" depends on** — the condition, not the escape.

The third is the real test. Almost everyone can state a view once. Holding it after someone pushes back is a different skill, and it is the one that gets noticed at work.

---

## What most people get wrong this week

**They pick opinions they actually hold, and only those.** Then the exercise becomes about being right, not about speaking. Days 30 and 31 deliberately make you argue things you do not believe, precisely so the content stops mattering.

**They apologise pre-emptively.** *"Sorry, I might be wrong, but maybe, I don't know…"* By the time the opinion arrives, the room has moved on. The apology is not politeness — it is a request for permission you do not need.

**They treat a dropped score as failure.** Week 5 averages usually rise. Harder work, worse numbers. That is the correct shape.
