# Week 6 — Professional English

> **At work, clarity outranks eloquence. Say less, land more.**

---

## The lie you have been believing

*"They'll take me less seriously because of my English."*

They take you less seriously because you go quiet, or because your point arrives buried in the fourth sentence after three qualifications.

Nobody in a meeting is grading your grammar. They are waiting to find out what you think and whether it is useful. A short, clear, slightly wrong sentence lands. A long, careful, perfect one does not — because by the time it finishes, the room has moved on.

---

## What this week does to you

Everything gets shorter and more structured.

Until now, longer answers were progress. This week, longer answers are the problem. You will practise the sixty-second update, the ten-second introduction, the four-sentence answer — because professional English is a compression skill, and compression is harder than expansion.

You will also learn to say "I don't know" without it sounding like a collapse.

## Weekly challenge

**Speak first, unprompted, in one real meeting.**

Not answering when asked. Speaking before anyone invites you.

A question counts. A summary counts — *"so are we saying we'll do X?"* is one of the most valuable sentences in any meeting and costs almost no English.

Speaking first matters because the longer you stay silent in a meeting, the higher the bar becomes for your first contribution. Go early, while the bar is low.

---

## Exit criteria

By the end of Day 42 you should be able to do all four:

- [ ] **Give a sixty-second update** with no filler and no backtracking.
- [ ] **Interrupt politely** and get the floor.
- [ ] **Answer an interview question** with a structure, not a ramble.
- [ ] **Say "I don't know"** and follow it with something useful.

The last one carries the most weight. Everyone eventually meets a question they cannot answer. What you do in the four seconds after is what people remember.

---

## What most people get wrong this week

**They add words to sound more professional.** *"I would like to just quickly mention that perhaps we could potentially consider…"* is eleven words of nothing. Professional English is shorter than casual English, not longer.

**They avoid interrupting because it feels rude.** In most meetings, never interrupting means never speaking. There is a polite way to do it, and Day 36 drills it.

**They treat "I don't know" as a failure state.** It is a normal sentence. What follows it is where the value is, and Day 41 exists entirely for that.
