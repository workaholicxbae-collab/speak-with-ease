# Week 7 — Pressure & Spontaneity

> **You will never feel ready. Trained recovery beats preparation.**

---

## The lie you have been believing

*"If I prepare enough, I won't freeze."*

You have been preparing for years and you still freeze, because preparation only covers the conversation you predicted. The moment reality goes somewhere else, the preparation becomes another thing to lose.

Worse: preparing teaches you that speaking requires preparation. So the unprepared moments — which is most of them — feel illegitimate, like you have been caught out.

The people who never freeze are not better prepared. They are better at recovering.

---

## What this week does to you

Preparation gets taken away, deliberately and repeatedly.

Fifteen seconds before a two-minute speech. Five topics in a row with no break. Interruptions mid-sentence. Background noise. And on Day 47, mistakes made on purpose.

Your scores will probably be the worst of the program this week. That is the design. A skill only counts if it survives conditions you did not choose.

## Weekly challenge

**Volunteer for something you would normally decline.**

Speak in a meeting you would usually sit out. Offer to explain something to a group. Take the call you would have pushed to email.

The point is not the task. It is doing the thing before you feel ready — which is the only condition under which you will ever do it, because the ready feeling does not arrive.

---

## Exit criteria

By the end of Day 49 you should be able to do all four:

- [ ] **Speak for two minutes** on an unseen topic with fifteen seconds of preparation.
- [ ] **Continue after an interruption** without restarting your sentence.
- [ ] **Keep talking through a mistake** without stopping to fix it.
- [ ] **Handle a hostile follow-up** without retreating into vagueness.

The third is the one that generalises. Once a mistake stops triggering a full stop, every other pressure gets easier — because most of what you experience as pressure is really the fear of a visible error.

---

## What most people get wrong this week

**They prepare anyway.** Fifteen seconds becomes thirty, becomes a quick note. The constraint is the exercise; loosening it removes the entire point.

**They treat the low scores as regression.** Week 7 numbers are the worst in the program for almost everyone. You are not worse. The conditions are harder.

**They skip Day 47** because deliberately making mistakes feels absurd. Day 47 is the most important day of the week. You cannot desensitise to something you keep avoiding.
