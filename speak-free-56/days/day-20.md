# Day 20 — React, Don't Wait

**Week 3 · Everyday Conversation · ~20 minutes · Format: reaction drill**

## Why today matters

Someone tells you something. There is a pause while you build a proper response. By the time it is ready, the moment has gone and your reply lands cold.

A reaction costs two words and arrives instantly. It buys you the four seconds you need to build the real answer — and it is the single cheapest thing in this program.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say aloud, fast: *really? · no way · that's awful · nice one · seriously?*
2. Say each one twice more, meaning it.
3. Say aloud: *"Two words now beats ten words late."*

## The concept (2 min)

Reactions are not filler. They do three jobs at once.

They tell the speaker you are listening. They keep the rhythm alive. And they buy you thinking time that does not sound like thinking time.

Native speakers produce them constantly and unconsciously — "oh really", "god", "that's mad" — several times a minute. You have almost certainly been silent through all those moments, and the silence is what made the conversation feel like an interview.

## Today's work (13 min)

**The bank (2 min).** Say each five times aloud:

| Situation | Reaction |
|---|---|
| Good news | *That's brilliant · Nice one · About time* |
| Bad news | *That's awful · Oh no · That's rough* |
| Surprise | *No way · Seriously? · You're joking* |
| Agreement | *Exactly · Same here · Tell me about it* |
| Interest | *Really? · Go on · How come?* |

**Drill 1 — Speed reactions (4 min).** Read a statement aloud, then react **within one second**. Record the whole set. Use these:

*I lost my phone. · I got the job. · My flight's delayed six hours. · I'm moving to Canada. · The meeting's cancelled. · I've been up since four. · They gave me a refund. · My laptop died mid-presentation.*

Save as `day-20-reactions.m4a`.

**The rule: one second, maximum.** If you pause to choose a good reaction, use a worse one faster.

**Drill 2 — React then continue (5 min).** Same statements. React in one second, **then** add a question or comment. Save as `day-20-react-continue.m4a`.

This is the real pattern: reaction buys time, question hands the turn back.

**Review (2 min).** Play Drill 2. Mark any reaction that took longer than a second.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** both drills run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** ask them to tell you five things about their day and react to each within a second.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 20 — Reaction Feeder** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Drill 2** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Expression** | Did the reactions sound genuine or recited? |
| **Hesitation** | How many took longer than a second? |
| **Flow** | Did reaction-then-question run as one move? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

## Stretch (optional)

Go through a whole real conversation today reacting to everything before you answer anything. Notice whether the other person talks more.
