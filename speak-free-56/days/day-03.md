# Day 3 — The 60-Second Rule

**Week 1 · Break the Silence · ~21 minutes · Format: 60-second nonstop**

## Why today matters

You stop mid-sentence because you are hunting for the right word. Every time you stop, you teach yourself that stopping is what happens when a sentence gets hard.

Today you break that link. One ordinary object, sixty seconds, and stopping is not permitted. By the end you will have proof that you can keep going — which is the only thing standing between you and a real conversation.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Shake out both hands for ten seconds.
2. Count aloud from 1 to 20 as fast as you can, clearly. Twice.
3. Say aloud: *"I am going to talk for sixty seconds and I am not going to stop."* Say it twice, and mean it the second time.

## The concept (2 min)

Pick up a spoon. You think there is nothing to say about a spoon.

There is: what it is made of, its weight, its colour, what it is for, who made it, where you got it, when you last used it, what happens if you lose it, what a world without spoons looks like, whether you prefer this one to others.

You never run out of content. You run out of *nerve* — the moment it gets hard, you stop and blame the topic. Sixty seconds is long enough to reach that moment and get past it.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Round 1 (3 min).** Pick up any ordinary object within reach — a cup, a pen, a shoe.

1. Start a 60-second timer. Press record.
2. Talk about that object until the timer ends.
3. **The rule: you may repeat yourself. You may not stop.** Repetition is fine. "This is a cup, it is a cup, the cup is white" is fine. Silence over two seconds is not.
4. Save as `day-03-object-1.m4a`.

**Round 2 (3 min).** New object, same sixty seconds, same rule. Save as `day-03-object-2.m4a`.

**Round 3 (3 min).** New object. This time use four angles in order: **what it is · what it does · where it came from · what you think of it.** Save as `day-03-object-3.m4a`.

**Compare (4 min).** Play Round 1 and Round 3 back to back. Round 3 will be better — not because your English improved in nine minutes, but because structure buys you time.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** the three rounds above run entirely alone.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 3 — Curious Listener** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

**Upgrade — with a person:** hand someone an object and say: *"I'm doing a speaking exercise. Give me any object and time me for a minute. Don't help me if I struggle."*

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 3 only**, once. Score these three:

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Flow** | Did the sixty seconds hold together? |
| **Hesitation** | How often did you search for a word? |
| **Recovery** | When you got stuck, what happened next? |

If nothing went wrong at all, write `n/a` for Recovery. See [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md).

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log one word: did you complete all three rounds without stopping? `yes` or `no`. If `no`, run Day 3 again tomorrow with an `easy` prompt from [../resources/prompt-bank.md](../resources/prompt-bank.md) before moving on. This one matters.

## Stretch (optional)

Ninety seconds instead of sixty, on an object you can see but cannot touch — something across the room or out of the window.
