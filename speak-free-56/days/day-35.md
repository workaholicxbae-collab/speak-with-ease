# Day 35 — Position Piece

**Week 5 · Opinions & Disagreement · ~12 minutes · Format: recorded position and reflection**

## Why today matters

Six days of drills on positions that mostly did not matter. Today you take one that does.

Three minutes, recorded, on something you genuinely believe — using the frames from Day 29, the conviction from Day 30, the fairness from Day 31 and the condition-naming from Day 34.

## Warm-up (2 min)

No vocal warm-up. Instead:

Write one line in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md): **an opinion you have held back from saying out loud at work or with friends.** Just for you.

That is probably today's topic. If it is too raw, pick your second choice.

## The concept (2 min)

A position piece has four parts, and the third is the one people skip:

1. **The claim** — one sentence, no hedging.
2. **Why** — two reasons, with an example for at least one.
3. **The strongest objection** — stated fairly, then answered.
4. **The restatement** — the claim again, narrowed by what you just conceded.

Part three is what separates an opinion from a rant. Naming the best counterargument yourself makes you *harder* to argue with, not easier — you have already shown you know the ground.

## Today's work (6 min)

**Choose and outline (2 min).** Your topic. Then say aloud, in note form only: the claim, two reasons, the objection, the restatement. Do not write full sentences.

**Record (3 min).** Three minutes, one take, no notes in front of you. Save as `day-35-position.m4a`.

**The rules:** no apology before the claim · name the strongest objection, not the weakest · restate at the end, narrowed if it needs narrowing.

**Listen (1 min).** Once. Mark whether all four parts were actually there. Most first attempts drop part three under time pressure.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** today is solo by design. This one is yours.

**Optional — with a person:** play it to someone who disagrees and ask one question — *"did I state your side fairly?"* That is the only feedback worth having on a position piece.

## Record & review (2 min)

Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Were all four parts present and in order? |
| **Expression** | Did you sound like you meant it? |
| **Flow** | Did three minutes hold without stalling? |

**Average the week (1 min).** Add Days 29–35, divide by seven, log as **Week 5 average**.

**Expect this to be worse than Week 4.** Holding a position while producing English is two jobs at once, and the number reflects the difficulty, not a loss of skill. A Week 5 rise is the normal shape. If your average *fell*, you either had an unusually good week or you have been choosing safe topics.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it.

Then check the weekly challenge: have you disagreed out loud with a real person this week? If not, you have until tonight.

## Stretch (optional)

Record a second position piece arguing the opposite of the first, with equal conviction. You now have both, and comparing them is unusually instructive.

## Tomorrow

Week 6. The stakes become real — this is your job. Clarity starts to matter more than range, and saying less becomes the harder skill.
