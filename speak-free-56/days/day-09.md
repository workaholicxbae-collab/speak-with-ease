# Day 9 — Word Sprints

**Week 2 · Think in English · ~20 minutes · Format: 60-second nonstop**

## Why today matters

On Day 3 you talked about an object you were holding. You could see it. It gave you something to describe.

Today there is no object. One word, and you go — which removes the last piece of scaffolding and tests whether the sixty seconds is actually yours.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say ten nouns as fast as you can. Any ten.
2. Pick the last one. Say three sentences about it, immediately.
3. Say aloud: *"I don't need a good first sentence. I need a first sentence."*

## The concept (2 min)

The hardest part of speaking is not the middle. It is the first three seconds, when there is nothing yet and you have to make something from nothing.

Most people fill that gap by searching for a *good* opening. That search is the freeze.

The fix is to accept a bad opening. "So, a chair. A chair is something you sit on." Nobody remembers your first sentence. They remember whether you kept going.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Get your words (1 min).** Open [../resources/prompt-bank.md](../resources/prompt-bank.md) and take three `easy` prompts — or point at three random nouns in any book. Do not read them properly yet. Do not choose the comfortable ones.

**Sprint 1 (3 min).** Look at word one. Start the timer and record immediately — no thinking pause at all. 60 seconds. Save as `day-09-sprint-1.m4a`.

**Sprint 2 (3 min).** Word two. Same. Save as `day-09-sprint-2.m4a`.

**Sprint 3 (3 min).** Word three. Same. Save as `day-09-sprint-3.m4a`.

**The rule: the recording starts before you know your first sentence.** Press record, *then* look at the word. The gap between seeing it and speaking is the thing being trained.

**Review (3 min).** Play all three. Time how long each took to get going. That number — the launch gap — should shrink across the three, and it is the only measure that matters today.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** the three sprints run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** have someone shout a random word and start the timer. Being handed the word by another human removes any chance of quietly pre-planning.

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Sprint 3** only. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Flow** | Did the sixty seconds hold? |
| **Recovery** | When you ran dry mid-sprint, what happened? |
| **Expression** | Did you sound interested, or like you were surviving? |

Expression is the sleeper here. Most people sprint in a flat panic voice. Noticing that is the first step to losing it.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it, plus your three launch gaps in seconds.

## Stretch (optional)

Three more sprints on `medium` prompts, 90 seconds each. Abstract words are far harder to launch from than objects, and that is exactly the point.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow you are banned from naming things. Ten objects, and you may not say a single one of their names.
