# Day 13 — One Photo, Two Minutes

**Week 2 · Think in English · ~21 minutes · Format: image-prompt storytelling**

## Why today matters

Everything you have said this fortnight has been true. Your morning, your objects, your opinions — all retrieved from memory.

Today none of it is true. You invent, which means you cannot pre-translate, because the sentence does not exist anywhere until you say it.

Invention is the fastest route to direct English there is.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Look at any object and invent one false fact about it. Say it aloud.
2. Invent two more. Say them with total confidence.
3. Say aloud: *"None of this has to be true."*

## The concept (2 min)

Recall and invention use different machinery.

When you recall, the content already exists and you translate it into English. When you invent, the content is *created in whatever language you are speaking* — there is no original to translate from.

This is why children learning a second language make things up constantly. It is not a distraction from the work. It is the shortcut.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Find a photo (1 min).** Any image with at least one person in it and some context. Search "street photography" or open a news site. **It must be a picture you have never seen before.**

**Round 1 — What is happening (4 min).** Record 2 minutes: who these people are, what just happened, what they want, what happens next. Invent all of it. Save as `day-13-photo-1.m4a`.

**The rule: never say "maybe" or "I think".** Not *"maybe she is a doctor"* — **"she is a doctor, and she is late."** Hedging is where translation sneaks back in.

**Round 2 — Different story (4 min).** Same photo. Completely different story. Different names, different reason, different ending. Save as `day-13-photo-2.m4a`.

The second telling is the real exercise. Your first story is now blocking the way, and you have to build past it.

**Round 3 — One minute, new photo (4 min).** New image, 60 seconds, one take, no thinking pause. Save as `day-13-photo-3.m4a`.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** all three rounds run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** have them pick the photo and hide it until you press record. Then tell them the story and let them ask two questions about your invented people.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 13 — Story Interrogator** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 2** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Flow** | Did the invented story move as freely as a true one? |
| **Expression** | Did you commit to it, or hedge? |
| **Clarity** | Could a listener follow who was who? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Most people hesitate *less* when inventing than when recalling. If that was true for you, write it down — it is direct evidence that translation, not vocabulary, has been the bottleneck.

## Stretch (optional)

Tell the same photo's story three times: once as a comedy, once as a tragedy, once as a news report. Same image, three registers.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow is lighter. You re-record the Day 1 prompt and play them back to back — the first real measurement of the fortnight.
