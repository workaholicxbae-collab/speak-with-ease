# Day 24 — Three Lengths

**Week 4 · Storytelling & Personality · ~22 minutes · Format: story retell at three lengths**

## Why today matters

Someone asks about your weekend in a lift. You have forty seconds. You either give two flat words or start something you cannot finish before the doors open.

Length is a choice, and today you learn to make it deliberately. The same story, three durations, each complete.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Describe your job in one sentence. Now in three. Now in ten.
2. Notice what you added and what you protected.
3. Say aloud: *"Short is not less. Short is chosen."*

## The concept (2 min)

Most people think a short version is the long version, rushed. It is not. Each length has a different job.

**30 seconds** — the turn only. What changed, and how it ended. No setup.
**90 seconds** — the spine, one beat per line, plus one detail.
**3 minutes** — the spine, detail throughout, and your opinion of what happened.

The mistake is treating the long version as the real one. In practice, the thirty-second version is the one you will use most, and it is the hardest to build.

## Today's work (14 min)

Use **any story** from Day 22 or 23.

**Version A — 3 minutes (5 min).** Full telling. Spine, detail, and what you make of it now. Save as `day-24-long.m4a`.

**Version B — 90 seconds (4 min).** Same story. One sentence per spine beat, plus one detail you refuse to lose. Save as `day-24-medium.m4a`.

**Version C — 30 seconds (3 min).** Same story. **Start at the turn.** No background. *"So I got locked out of my own flat at midnight in the rain…"* Save as `day-24-short.m4a`.

**The rule: each version must be complete.** Version C is not the first thirty seconds of Version A. It is a whole story that happens to be short.

**Compare (2 min).** Play C then A. Notice what A contains that C did not need. That surplus is where you lose people in real conversations.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** all three versions run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** tell them Version C only. If they ask a follow-up question, it worked — a good short version leaves a hook.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 24 — Length Editor** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Version C** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Complete in thirty seconds, or just truncated? |
| **Flow** | Did compression make you rush? |
| **Expression** | Did the short version still sound like a story? |

Rushing is the common failure. Compression means cutting content, not speaking faster — and speaking faster is what everyone does first.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log which version was hardest. It is almost always C, which is worth knowing given C is the one real life asks for.

## Stretch (optional)

Ten seconds. One sentence, the whole story. Brutal, and the fastest way to find out whether you know what your story is actually about.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow you aim for a reaction. Something that went wrong, told so the ending lands — and timing turns out to be mechanical, not magical.
