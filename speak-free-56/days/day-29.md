# Day 29 — Opinion Frames

**Week 5 · Opinions & Disagreement · ~20 minutes · Format: phrase-frame drill**

## Why today matters

You have opinions. What you lack is a way to launch them that does not feel either arrogant or apologetic — so you say nothing and later think of exactly what you meant.

Today you get ten openings. Drilled, so they arrive without a search.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say aloud: *"I think the best day of the week is Wednesday."* Say it like you mean it.
2. Now: *"I'd argue Wednesday is the best day of the week."* Feel the difference.
3. Say aloud: *"I'm allowed to be wrong out loud."*

## The concept (2 min)

A frame does two things at once: it commits you to a position and signals how strongly you hold it.

That second part is what you have been missing. Without a frame, every opinion sounds either like a fact you are asserting or a guess you are ashamed of. With one, you can say *"from what I've seen"* and be clear that this is your experience, not a claim about the world.

Frames are not softeners. They are calibration.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Something to listen to:** [video library](../resources/video-library.md#opinions-and-disagreement--days-2934) — take the first clip that fits. Do not browse.

**The ten frames (2 min).** Read aloud once:

| Strength | Frame |
|---|---|
| Strong | *I'd argue… · I'm convinced… · The way I see it…* |
| Medium | *From what I've seen… · In my experience… · I tend to think…* |
| Exploring | *I'm not sure yet, but… · My instinct is… · It seems to me…* |
| Conditional | *It depends on whether…* |

**Drill (5 min).** Take any `medium` prompt from [../resources/prompt-bank.md](../resources/prompt-bank.md). Say **one opinion using each of the ten frames** — ten different sentences on the same topic.

Same view, ten launches. You are training the opening, not the argument.

**Record (4 min).** Three `medium` prompts. For each, give a 45-second opinion, **starting with a frame**. Save as `day-29-frames.m4a`.

**The rule: the frame comes first, and no apology before it.** Not *"sorry, I don't know much about this, but I'd argue…"* Just *"I'd argue…"*

**Review (2 min).** Play it back. Mark any opinion that arrived without a frame, and any apology that crept in front of one.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** the drill and recording run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** state three opinions to them using frames. Watch whether they respond to the opinion or to your uncertainty.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 29 — Frame Spotter** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Was your actual position obvious within one sentence? |
| **Expression** | Did you sound like you meant it? |
| **Hesitation** | Did the frame arrive without a search? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log how many apologies crept in before your opinions. Most people are surprised by the count.

## Stretch (optional)

Use `hard` prompts. Abstract topics pull the hedging straight back out, which tells you which frames are actually automatic yet.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow you defend something trivial with total conviction — and discover that conviction is a delivery choice, not a state of certainty.
