# Day 2 — Borrowed Fluency

**Week 1 · Break the Silence · ~22 minutes · Format: shadowing**

## Why today matters

You have never heard fluent English come out of your own mouth. That is the actual problem — not grammar, not vocabulary. Your body has no memory of what fluency feels like from the inside.

Today you get that memory. You will not invent the sentences, so the part of your brain that checks and freezes has nothing to do. It stands down, and your mouth finally moves at full speed.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Yawn wide three times.
2. Say "bah bah bah bah" fast, ten times. Then "dah dah dah dah". Then "gah gah gah gah".
3. Read this aloud twice, at speed: *"Red lorry, yellow lorry, red lorry, yellow lorry."*

Your mouth is a set of muscles. It is now warm.

## The concept (2 min)

Shadowing means speaking *along with* a recording, one or two words behind, copying everything — speed, rhythm, where they pause, where they push.

You are not translating. You are not composing. You are copying, the way a musician learns a piece by playing along before they ever improvise.

The fluency you feel today is borrowed. That is fine. Borrowed fluency is how every fluent speaker started, and your mouth cannot tell the difference — it only knows it just moved at native speed and nothing bad happened.

## Today's work (14 min)

**Find your clip (2 min).** Any 2-minute stretch of [clear conversational English](../resources/video-library.md#shadowing--days-2-24) — podcast, interview, video. Two conditions: one person talking, and you understand it without subtitles. **Not a news reader** — that rhythm is artificial and teaches the wrong thing.

**Pass 1 — Listen (2 min).** Play it once, silently. Notice where they pause and which words they stress.

**Pass 2 — Whisper (3 min).** Play again, whispering one or two words behind. You will fall off. Rejoin at the next sentence — never rewind.

**Pass 3 — Full voice (3 min).** Same clip, normal volume. Copy their speed even when it feels too fast.

**Pass 4 — Record (3 min).** Shadow once more, recording **yourself only**. Save as `day-02-shadowing.m4a`.

**The rule: match their rhythm, not their accent.** You are copying the music, not the voice. Your accent is not being changed today or any other day.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** the clip is your partner. This day is built to run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** ask someone to read a paragraph aloud slowly while you shadow them live. Harder, because humans are less predictable than recordings.

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to `day-02-shadowing.m4a` **once**. Score these three only:

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Flow** | Did it move without stopping? |
| **Expression** | Did you copy their emphasis, or flatten it? |
| **Hesitation** | Did you search, or stay with them? |

Full descriptions in [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md).

Expect Flow and Expression to be **higher than anything you would produce alone**. That gap is today's lesson: the ability was already in your mouth. Only the composing step was slowing you down.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Most people score noticeably lower than Day 1. If yours dropped by three or more, write that down — you will want to see it on Day 7.

## Stretch (optional)

Shadow the same clip once more with your eyes closed and no text. Then, immediately after, say the same content in your own words for 60 seconds. Notice how much of their rhythm stayed with you.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow the words are yours. Sixty seconds, one ordinary object, and one rule that makes stopping impossible.
