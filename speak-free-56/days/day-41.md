# Day 41 — "I Don't Know"

**Week 6 · Professional English · ~21 minutes · Format: pressure response drill**

## Why today matters

Someone asks a question you cannot answer. You freeze, apologise, and either invent something vague or go silent — and afterwards you decide it was your English that failed.

It was not. Native speakers get asked things they do not know constantly. They just have a sentence ready, and today you get one.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say aloud: *"I don't know."* Three times. Flat, no apology.
2. Say: *"I don't know, but I can find out by Thursday."* Three times.
3. Say aloud: *"Not knowing is normal. Freezing is the problem."*

## The concept (2 min)

"I don't know" is a complete, professional sentence. What ruins it is what people put around it — apology in front, silence behind.

Four ways to finish it well:

**Commit** — *"I don't know, but I'll find out by Thursday."*
**Redirect** — *"I don't know that part, but what I can tell you is…"*
**Narrow** — *"I don't know overall, but for our team specifically, yes."*
**Ask** — *"I don't know — what's making you ask?"*

That last one is the most useful and the least used. It hands the turn back, buys you time, and usually reveals what they actually wanted.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Drill (3 min).** Say all four endings aloud, five times each. Twenty repetitions.

**Round 1 — Impossible questions (5 min).** Record yourself answering eight questions you genuinely cannot answer. Invent them about your work, or use these:

*What's the exact figure for last quarter? · Why did the previous team choose this? · What will this cost in three years? · Who signed that off? · What's the industry benchmark? · How does the competitor do it? · What happens if it fails at scale? · When was this last reviewed?*

Use a different ending each time. Save as `day-41-idk.m4a`.

**The rules:** never apologise before "I don't know" · never invent an answer · never trail into silence.

**Round 2 — Under pressure (5 min).** Same, but the other role responds with *"You should know that"* or *"That's not good enough."* Hold steady and repeat your sentence without escalating. Save as `day-41-pressure.m4a`.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** both rounds run alone, both roles.

**Upgrade — with a person:** ask them to interrogate you about your work and be dissatisfied with every answer.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 41 — Unsatisfied Questioner** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 2** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Recovery** | After the pushback, did you stay level? |
| **Clarity** | Was it clear what you *could* offer? |
| **Expression** | Did you sound composed or defensive? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log how many times you apologised before saying you did not know. That habit is deeply set, and counting it is the fastest way to break it.

## Stretch (optional)

Have someone ask you fifteen questions where only four are answerable, in a random order. Not knowing which is which is the realistic version.
