# Day 4 — Kill the Um

**Week 1 · Break the Silence · ~22 minutes · Format: constraint drill with measurement**

## Why today matters

"Um" is not a speech habit. It is a noise you make to hold your turn while you translate — a signal that says *don't take the floor, I'm still working.*

It also tells every listener exactly how uncertain you feel. Today you swap it for silence. A speaker who pauses sounds like they are thinking. A speaker who says "ummmm" sounds like they are drowning. The gap is identical in length. Only the sound differs.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Breathe in for four counts, out for six. Four times.
2. Say one sentence about your day. Then say it again with a deliberate two-second silence in the middle. Feel how long two seconds is — it is shorter than you think.
3. Say aloud: *"Silence is allowed. Searching out loud is not."*

## The concept (2 min)

There are two kinds of gap.

**The thinking pause** — you stop, the room is quiet, you choose your next word, you continue. Listeners read this as competence.

**The searching noise** — "ummm", "aaah", "you know", "how to say". Same gap, filled with sound. Listeners read this as struggle.

You are not removing the pause. You could not if you wanted to; thinking takes time. You are removing the *sound* you make during it.

## Today's work (14 min)

**Take 1 — Count the damage (5 min).**

1. Pick any `easy` prompt from [../resources/prompt-bank.md](../resources/prompt-bank.md).
2. Record 2 minutes. Speak normally — do not try to avoid anything.
3. Save as `day-04-take-1.m4a`.
4. Play it back with a pen. Mark **every** filler: "um", "ah", "like", "you know", and any word in your first language.
5. Write the total. Most people score 15–40. A high number is a measurement, not a failure.

**Take 2 — Replace with silence (6 min).**

6. Same prompt. Record 2 minutes again.
7. **The rule: every time a filler is coming, close your mouth and let the silence happen instead.** Do not speed up to avoid the gap. Do not stop talking altogether. Just let it be quiet, then continue.
8. Save as `day-04-take-2.m4a`.
9. Play back and count again.

**Compare (3 min).** Write both numbers side by side in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Take 2 will be lower and will also feel *worse* while you are doing it. Silence feels enormous from the inside and normal from the outside. That mismatch is the thing to learn today.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** both takes run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** ask someone to sit with you and raise a hand every time you use a filler. Live feedback is harsher and faster than counting on playback.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 4 — Filler Counter** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Take 2** once. Score these three:

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Hesitation** | How much searching remained? |
| **Flow** | Did the silences break the speech, or sit inside it? |
| **Clarity** | Would a stranger have followed it? |

See [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md).

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it, along with both filler counts.

Those two numbers get compared again on Day 49. Write them where you will find them.

## Stretch (optional)

Take 3: two minutes on a `medium` prompt, silence instead of filler, and no restarting. Harder prompts pull fillers back out of you — that is worth seeing now rather than in Week 5.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow the recording stops being private. You send English to another human being, and they reply.
