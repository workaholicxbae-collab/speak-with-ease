# Day 34 — Nuance

**Week 5 · Opinions & Disagreement · ~20 minutes · Format: nuance Q&A**

## Why today matters

"It depends" is the most useful phrase in professional English and the most abused. Said alone it is an escape hatch — it sounds thoughtful while committing to nothing, and everyone in the room knows it.

Said properly, it is the most senior-sounding thing you can say. The difference is one word: **on**.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say aloud: *"It depends."* Notice how empty it is.
2. Say: *"It depends on how much time we have."* Notice the difference.
3. Say aloud: *"It depends on — and then I name the thing."*

## The concept (2 min)

The full pattern has three parts:

> **It depends on [the condition].** If [X], then [answer A]. If [Y], then [answer B].

*"It depends on the team size. Under five people, do it manually. Above that, the manual version breaks."*

That is not fence-sitting. It is two clear positions and the rule for choosing between them — which is more useful than either answer alone, and it is exactly how experienced people talk.

Naming the condition is the whole skill. Everything else follows.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Drill (3 min).** Take six questions — `medium` or `hard` from [../resources/prompt-bank.md](../resources/prompt-bank.md). For each, say only: *"It depends on…"* and finish the sentence. Do not answer the question yet.

Six conditions in three minutes. Speed matters here.

**Round 1 — Full pattern (5 min).** Same six questions. Record. For each, give the complete three-part answer: the condition, then both branches. About 45 seconds each. Save as `day-34-nuance.m4a`.

**The rule: you must commit to both branches.** Not *"if X, then maybe something like A"* — say what A is. "It depends" is only earned if what follows is specific.

**Round 2 — Under pressure (3 min).** Take the hardest of the six. Answer with the full pattern, then have the other role say: *"Fine — but if you had to pick one, which?"* Answer that too, in one sentence. Save as `day-34-pressed.m4a`.

This is the follow-up that exposes people who use "it depends" as cover. You should be able to pick.

**Review (2 min).** Play Round 1. Mark any answer where you named no condition.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** both rounds run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** have them ask the six questions and press you for a single answer each time.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 34 — Condition Presser** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 2** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Was the condition named specifically? |
| **Recovery** | When pressed to pick one, did you? |
| **Flow** | Did the three-part pattern run smoothly? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log how many of the six had a genuinely specific condition. Vague conditions — "it depends on the situation" — do not count.

## Stretch (optional)

Use the pattern on a question about your own work where you genuinely do not know the answer. Naming the condition is often more valuable than having one.
