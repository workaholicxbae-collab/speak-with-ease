# Day 37 — Three Introductions

**Week 6 · Professional English · ~21 minutes · Format: three-length introduction**

## Why today matters

"So, tell me about yourself." You either give your name and job title and stop dead, or you talk for four minutes and watch them lose interest.

The problem is not content. It is that you have one introduction and three different situations.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say your name and what you do. Ten seconds. Stop.
2. Say it again with one thing you are working on now.
3. Say aloud: *"Different rooms need different lengths."*

## The concept (2 min)

Three versions, three jobs:

**Ten seconds** — a name and a handle. *"I'm Priya, I do data work for a logistics company."* Used at introductions and on calls. Its only job is to let the conversation continue.

**Thirty seconds** — role, one thing you are working on, one thing you are known for. Used when someone actually asks about your work.

**Ninety seconds** — the arc. Where you started, what changed, where you are now, what you want next. Used in interviews and only there.

The most common error is using the ninety in a ten-second situation. It reads as nervousness, not thoroughness.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Something to listen to:** [video library](../resources/video-library.md#interviews--days-37-38-42) — take the first clip that fits. Do not browse.

**Build the ten (3 min).** Write nothing. Say it aloud six times, adjusting each time until it fits in ten seconds and sounds like speech.

Record the final version. Save as `day-37-ten.m4a`.

**Build the thirty (4 min).** Role, current work, one thing you are good at. Say it aloud four times, then record. Save as `day-37-thirty.m4a`.

**The rule: no job titles that need explaining.** If your title means nothing outside your company, say what you do instead. "I'm a Senior Delivery Lead II" tells a stranger nothing.

**Build the ninety (5 min).** The arc — started here, this changed, now I do this, next I want that.

Use the Day 22 spine. It is the same shape. Say it twice, then record. Save as `day-37-ninety.m4a`.

**Compare (1 min).** Play the ten and the ninety back to back. They should sound like the same person, not two different scripts.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** all three versions run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** give them the thirty and ask what they think you do. If they cannot say it back, it did not land.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 37 — Introduction Tester** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to the **thirty-second version** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Would a stranger know what you do? |
| **Expression** | Did you sound interested in your own work? |
| **Flow** | Did it run without restarting? |

Expression is the giveaway. Most people describe their job in a voice that suggests they are apologising for it.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log which length was hardest. It is usually the ten — compression again.

## Stretch (optional)

Record a fourth version for someone outside your industry entirely. No jargon at all, and no reliance on them knowing what your company does.
