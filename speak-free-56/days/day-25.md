# Day 25 — Timing & Humour

**Week 4 · Storytelling & Personality · ~21 minutes · Format: comic timing drill**

## Why today matters

You are funny in your first language and flat in English. So you have concluded that humour requires fluency you do not have.

It does not. Humour requires **timing** — where you pause, what you hold back, which word lands last. Timing is mechanical, and mechanical things can be drilled.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say: *"I got to the airport… and realised I'd left my passport at home."* Now say it again with a two-second pause at the dots.
2. Say it a third time, pausing and then dropping your voice on the last word.
3. Say aloud: *"The pause does the work."*

## The concept (2 min)

Three mechanics, no cleverness required:

**Put the surprise last.** *"I found my keys — in the fridge"* beats *"I found my keys in the fridge, which was strange."* Once the surprise has landed, stop talking.

**Pause before it.** One or two seconds. The silence tells the listener something is coming.

**Underplay the ending.** The more casually you deliver the strange part, the funnier it is. Explaining it kills it.

That is the whole technique. Nothing here needs advanced English.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Choose (1 min).** Something that went wrong for you. Mildly — a mistake, a misunderstanding, a small disaster. Not a tragedy and not a triumph.

**Round 1 — Flat (3 min).** Tell it in 60 seconds with no technique at all. Deliberately dull. Save as `day-25-flat.m4a`.

**Round 2 — Reordered (4 min).** Same story, but move the surprising thing to the **last sentence**. Everything before it becomes setup. Save as `day-25-ordered.m4a`.

**Round 3 — With pauses (3 min).** Same order, now add a two-second silence before the ending, and deliver the ending quietly. Save as `day-25-timed.m4a`.

**The rule: stop talking the moment the ending lands.** No explaining. No "so that was funny". No nervous laugh. Silence is the finish.

**Compare (2 min).** Play Round 1 and Round 3 back to back. Same words, mostly. Completely different effect.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** all three rounds run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** tell them Round 3 and watch. You are looking for the half-second gap before they react — that gap means the pause worked.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 25 — Timing Audience** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 3** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Expression** | Was the ending underplayed or oversold? |
| **Flow** | Did the setup move without dragging? |
| **Clarity** | Was the surprise actually the last thing said? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log whether you stopped talking after the ending, or explained it. Explaining it is the near-universal first-attempt error.

## Stretch (optional)

Take a story that is not funny at all and give it comic timing anyway. Notice that timing alone makes a dull story listenable.
