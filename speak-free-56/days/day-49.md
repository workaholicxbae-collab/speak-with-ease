# Day 49 — Under the Clock

**Week 7 · Pressure & Spontaneity · ~12 minutes · Format: timed pressure recording and reflection**

## Why today matters

The hardest week ends with a measurement, and one of them goes back forty-five days.

On Day 4 you counted your filler words. Today you count them again under far worse conditions than Day 4 ever imposed — and that comparison is the cleanest number in the program.

## Warm-up (2 min)

No vocal warm-up. Instead:

Find your **Day 4 filler counts** in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md) — both takes. Write them at the top of today's row.

Do not try to remember them. Look them up. Memory will flatter you.

## The concept (2 min)

Week 7 conditions have been deliberately unfair: no preparation, interruptions, fatigue, noise, deliberate errors.

Today's recording happens under time pressure with no preparation. If your filler count is at or below Day 4's, that is a genuine result — because Day 4 was two easy minutes on a familiar prompt in a quiet room, and today is not.

Compare across conditions, not just across numbers.

## Today's work (6 min)

**Set up (1 min).** Take a `hard` prompt from [../resources/prompt-bank.md](../resources/prompt-bank.md). Do not read it yet.

**The recording (2 min).** Read the prompt. **Five seconds** of preparation. Then speak for exactly two minutes.

Save as `day-49-pressure.m4a`.

**The rules:** one take · no restarts · no self-correction · stop at exactly two minutes.

**Count (2 min).** Play it back with a pen. Mark every filler — *um, ah, like, you know*, and any word in your first language.

Write the total next to your two Day 4 numbers.

**Compare (1 min).** Three numbers now: Day 4 normal, Day 4 with silences, Day 49 under pressure.

Most people land near or below their Day 4 second take — while doing something considerably harder. That is what progress looks like when it is measured honestly.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** today is solo by design.

**Optional — with a person:** have them count your fillers live. A second counter is more accurate than you are, because you stop hearing your own.

## Record & review (2 min)

Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Flow** | Did two minutes hold under time pressure? |
| **Hesitation** | How does the count compare with Day 4? |
| **Recovery** | After the worst moment, did you rebuild? |

**Average the week (1 min).** Add Days 43–49, divide by seven, log as **Week 7 average**.

**Expect the worst average of the program.** Every day this week removed something you had been relying on. A high number here means you were doing the work as designed. If Week 7 was your *best* week, check honestly whether you kept the timers.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it.

## Stretch (optional)

Record a second two minutes on a different `hard` prompt with **zero** preparation, and count again. Two data points under pressure beat one.

## Tomorrow

The last week. You stop drilling and build one thing — a real talk, on a topic you care about, delivered to a real human being on Day 55.
