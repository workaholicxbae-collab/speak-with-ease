# Day 10 — Describe, Don't Translate

**Week 2 · Think in English · ~21 minutes · Format: describe-don't-translate**

## Why today matters

You hit a word you do not know. You stop, you search your first language, you try to translate, you fail, you apologise.

Fluent speakers in a second language hit missing words constantly. They just go around them, and nobody notices. Today you learn the going-around, which is the single most useful survival skill in this program.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Point at three things and describe each without naming it. Ten seconds each.
2. Say aloud: *"The thing you use to…"* and finish it three different ways.
3. Say: *"I don't need the word. I need the meaning."*

## The concept (2 min)

When a word is missing, you have four moves. Only the last one is a failure.

- **Function** — *"the thing you use to open bottles"*
- **Category plus detail** — *"a kind of small tool, metal, fits in your hand"*
- **Comparison** — *"like a knife, but not sharp"*
- **Switch languages or stop** — the one we are removing

The first three keep the conversation alive. Native speakers use them too, constantly, whenever a name escapes them. This is not a beginner's workaround; it is how speaking works.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Round 1 — Ten objects (6 min).** Choose ten objects around you. Record yourself describing each one for about 30 seconds, **without ever saying its name**.

Use the three moves above. Save as `day-10-objects.m4a`.

**The rule: if the name slips out, stop that object and move to the next one.** No second attempts. The penalty keeps you alert.

**Round 2 — Hard mode (4 min).** Five abstract things: *a promise · being late · jealousy · a deadline · privacy*. Same rule, about 30 seconds each. Save as `day-10-abstract.m4a`.

Abstract nouns cannot be described by function, so you are forced into comparison and example. This is the harder skill and the one you will actually use at work.

**Review (3 min).** Play back Round 2. Count how many times you nearly reached for your first language.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** both rounds run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** describe objects while they guess. Correct guesses mean your description worked. This is the fastest feedback in the whole program.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 10 — Guess the Object** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 2** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Could a stranger have guessed what you meant? |
| **Recovery** | When you got stuck, did you go around or stall? |
| **Flow** | Did the descriptions move, or crawl? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log how many objects you completed without the name slipping out, out of fifteen.

## Stretch (optional)

Describe your own job for two minutes without using a single technical term from your field. Harder than it sounds, and directly useful in Week 6.
