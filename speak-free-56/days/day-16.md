# Day 16 — Counter Roleplay

**Week 3 · Everyday Conversation · ~22 minutes · Format: two-role simulation**

## Why today matters

There is a specific list of interactions you avoid. Ordering something complicated. Asking for a refund. Changing a booking on the phone.

You avoid them because they have unpredictable middles — you can plan the opening, but not what they say back.

Today you play both roles, which means you rehearse the unpredictable part too.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Order a coffee out loud. Now order it with a change: no sugar, extra hot.
2. Say the same order as an impatient customer, then as an apologetic one.
3. Say aloud: *"I am allowed to take up their time."*

## The concept (2 min)

Playing both sides works because it forces you to generate the reply you fear.

When you rehearse only your own lines, you plan for the version where everything goes smoothly. Then they say *"sorry, we don't do that"* and the plan is gone.

Voicing the other role makes you produce the awkward turns yourself — and once you have said the difficult sentence in your own voice, hearing it from a stranger stops being a surprise.

## Today's work (14 min)

Run three scenes. In each, **speak both parts aloud**, switching clearly — turn your head, or change your voice.

**Scene 1 — Café (4 min).** You order. They are out of what you want. You choose something else and ask a question about it. Record. Save as `day-16-cafe.m4a`.

**Scene 2 — Shop return (5 min).** You return something. They ask why, then say there is a problem — no receipt, past the date. You push politely. Save as `day-16-return.m4a`.

**Scene 3 — Ticket change (5 min).** You change a booking. They offer a worse option. You ask for alternatives and confirm the details back to them. Save as `day-16-ticket.m4a`.

**The rule: the other role must make it difficult at least once per scene.** A cooperative counter clerk teaches you nothing. If every scene goes smoothly, you are protecting yourself.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** all three scenes run alone, both roles.

**Upgrade — with a person:** hand them the difficult role and tell them explicitly to be unhelpful. People are too kind by default; you have to ask.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 16 — Difficult Counter Staff** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Scene 2** only. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Recovery** | When the other role refused, what happened next? |
| **Clarity** | Was your request unambiguous? |
| **Expression** | Did you sound like someone entitled to ask? |

Expression is the tell. Most learners ask for things in an apologetic voice that invites refusal, and hearing it once is usually enough to stop.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log which scene you found hardest. That is the interaction to do for real this week.

## Stretch (optional)

A fourth scene: complain about something. Not aggressively — clearly. State the problem, what you want, and what happens if it is not resolved.

Tomorrow you learn to keep a topic alive. Three questions deep, and the conversation does not die.
