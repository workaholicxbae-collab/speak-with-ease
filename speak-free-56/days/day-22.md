# Day 22 — The Story Spine

**Week 4 · Storytelling & Personality · ~21 minutes · Format: structure drill**

## Why today matters

You lose your way in the middle of stories. You start well, then realise you have forgotten something important, go back, and the story collapses into "anyway, it was funny at the time".

That is not an English problem. It is a navigation problem, and today you get a map.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say aloud what you did this morning in exactly three sentences. No more.
2. Now the same, in five sentences, with one thing that went wrong.
3. Say aloud: *"If I know the next line, I don't panic."*

## The concept (2 min)

Five lines hold up almost every story ever told:

> **Once upon a time…** — the normal situation
> **Every day…** — how things usually were
> **Until one day…** — the thing that changed
> **Because of that…** — what it caused
> **Finally…** — how it ended

You do not say these words aloud. They are internal rails. While you are speaking line three, you already know line four exists — and that knowledge is what stops the freeze.

The spine is not a template for children's stories. It is how a work update, a complaint and an anecdote are all shaped.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Fit an existing story (3 min).** Take any film you know well. Say its five spine lines aloud, one sentence each. Fifteen seconds total.

**Round 1 — Your story, bare spine (3 min).** Pick something real that happened to you in the last year. Say only the five lines. No detail. Record. Save as `day-22-spine.m4a`.

**Round 2 — Expand (4 min).** Same story, same five beats, but two or three sentences per beat. About 90 seconds total. Save as `day-22-expanded.m4a`.

**The rule: never leave a beat to go back.** If you forget something from beat two while you are in beat four, leave it out. The story survives a missing detail; it does not survive you reversing.

**Round 3 — Different story (3 min).** New event, straight to the expanded version, 90 seconds, one take. Save as `day-22-story-2.m4a`.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** all three rounds run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** tell them Round 3 and ask afterwards what changed in the story. If they cannot name the turning point, beat three was too weak.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 22 — Spine Checker** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 3** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Could a stranger name the turning point? |
| **Flow** | Did you move forward without reversing? |
| **Hesitation** | Did knowing the next beat reduce the searching? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Compare against Day 9's sprints. Most people hesitate noticeably less with a spine, on harder material. That difference is the value of structure, measured.

## Stretch (optional)

Put a work situation through the spine — a project, a problem, a result. This is exactly the shape of a good interview answer, and Week 6 will ask for it.
