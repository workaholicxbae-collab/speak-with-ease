# Day 51 — Architecture

**Week 8 · Own the Room · ~21 minutes · Format: talk architecture**

## Why today matters

Yesterday you had an outline. Today it becomes a structure you can walk through without notes.

The structure is what lets you speak without memorising. If you know where you are and what comes next, you can lose a sentence and stay standing — which is the entire difference between a talk and a recital.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say your claim from yesterday aloud. One sentence.
2. Say your three points aloud, in order, as three short phrases.
3. Say aloud: *"I learn the shape, not the words."*

## The concept (2 min)

Five parts, in this order:

> **Hook** — 30 seconds. A question, a specific moment, or a surprising claim. Never *"Today I'm going to talk about…"*
> **Claim** — 15 seconds. What you want them to believe.
> **Three points** — 3 minutes. About a minute each, each with one example.
> **The objection** — 30 seconds. The strongest thing against you, named and answered. This is Day 35's move, and it does the same work here.
> **Close** — 30 seconds. Return to the hook. Say what changes.

Five minutes. Anything that does not fit one of these five parts does not go in.

Returning to the hook at the close is the cheapest technique in public speaking and the one that makes a talk feel finished rather than merely stopped.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Build the hook (4 min).** Try three different openings aloud, 30 seconds each:

- A question to the audience
- A specific moment — *"Last March I watched a team spend six weeks on…"*
- A surprising claim

Record all three. Save as `day-51-hooks.m4a`. Pick the one that came out most naturally, not the cleverest.

**Build the three points (5 min).** Say each point aloud for one minute. Each needs **one example**, not three — examples are what people remember, and more than one per point crowds it out.

**Build the close (2 min).** Return to the hook. Answer it, resolve it, or complete it. Then say what you want to change.

**Run the skeleton (2 min).** Say the whole structure as headlines only — hook line, claim, three point-names, objection, close line. Under sixty seconds.

Save as `day-51-skeleton.m4a`. **This file is what you rehearse from. Not a script.**

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** the whole build runs alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** give them the three hooks and ask which made them want to hear more.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 51 — Hook Tester** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to the **skeleton** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Are all five parts distinguishable? |
| **Flow** | Did the structure come out in order without hunting? |
| **Expression** | Did the hook sound like an opening or a throat-clear? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log whether you can say the five-part skeleton from memory. If not, drill it once more before tomorrow — everything this week rests on it.

## Stretch (optional)

Write the talk out in full, then throw the paper away and deliver from the skeleton. Writing clarifies the thinking; keeping the paper wrecks the delivery.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow you leave the content alone entirely and work only on how it sounds. The same sixty seconds, three different ways.
