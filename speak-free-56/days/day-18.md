# Day 18 — Voice Only

**Week 3 · Everyday Conversation · ~21 minutes · Format: phone-call simulation**

## Why today matters

You have been leaning on things you cannot use on a phone: your hands, your face, the other person's expression telling you whether you are making sense.

Phone calls in a second language are harder than face-to-face for everyone. Today you practise the hard version deliberately, so that ordinary conversation starts to feel like it has extra help in it.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Sit on your hands. Describe your morning for thirty seconds without moving them.
2. Say your full name and phone number aloud, clearly, three times. This is the most commonly botched moment on any real call.
3. Say aloud: *"They cannot see me. Everything has to be in the words."*

## The concept (2 min)

On a call you lose about half your usual signal, so three things have to carry more weight.

**Structure** — say what you want in the first two sentences, not the last.
**Confirmation** — repeat back numbers, dates and names. *"So that's Tuesday the ninth, at four."*
**Verbal nodding** — "right", "okay", "I see". On a call, silence reads as a lost connection, not as listening.

That third one is the piece people forget, and it is why their calls feel awkward.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Something to listen to:** [video library](../resources/video-library.md#phone-and-transactional-english--days-16-18) — take the first clip that fits. Do not browse.

**Sit on your hands for all three scenes.** Physically. It matters more than it sounds.

**Call 1 — Appointment (4 min).** You call to reschedule. Give your name, your reason, your preferred time. They offer something worse. You negotiate and confirm the details back. Both roles, recorded. Save as `day-18-call-1.m4a`.

**Call 2 — Information (4 min).** You call a business with three questions. Ask all three, confirm each answer, thank them, end the call cleanly. Save as `day-18-call-2.m4a`.

**Call 3 — Problem (5 min).** Something has gone wrong — a delivery, a bill, a booking. Explain it, say what you want, handle one refusal. Save as `day-18-call-3.m4a`.

**The rule: confirm every number, date and name back.** Every one. This is the habit that survives into real calls.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** all three run alone, both roles, hands still.

**Upgrade — with a person:** sit back to back, out of each other's sight, and run Call 3. No visual signal at all.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 18 — Phone Line** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Call 3** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Was your problem stated in the first two sentences? |
| **Recovery** | After the refusal, did you continue or collapse? |
| **Flow** | Did the call move, or fill with dead air? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log whether you confirmed every number back. Most people miss at least one on the first attempt.

## Stretch (optional)

Make one real phone call in English today. A shop asking their closing time is enough. Real calls have real background noise and real impatience, and neither can be simulated.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow you leave the room. Three real minutes with a real human being — the whole week has been rehearsal for it.
