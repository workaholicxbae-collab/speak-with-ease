# Day 12 — Speed Round

**Week 2 · Think in English · ~20 minutes · Format: rapid-fire Q&A**

## Why today matters

In real conversation nobody gives you preparation time. The question arrives and the answer is expected immediately.

Today removes preparation entirely. Ten questions, thirty seconds each, no gaps, no restarts. By question six you will stop composing and start simply answering — which is the whole point.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Answer aloud, immediately: *What did you have for breakfast?* Then: *What is annoying you today?* Then: *What happens tomorrow?*
2. Notice you did not need to prepare any of those.
3. Say aloud: *"Thirty seconds is enough. It has to be."*

## The concept (2 min)

A short answer that arrives beats a complete answer that does not.

You have been treating every question as though a full, well-built response is required. It is not. Real answers are three or four sentences, they are slightly messy, and they land while the other person is still interested.

Thirty seconds is roughly four sentences. That is a real answer, not a compromise.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Prepare the list (1 min).** Take **ten** `easy` or `medium` prompts from [../resources/prompt-bank.md](../resources/prompt-bank.md). Do not read them. Write only the numbers down, so you meet each question cold.

**The round (8 min).** Press record once and leave it running for the whole set.

1. Read question one aloud. Answer for 30 seconds.
2. At 30 seconds, move immediately to question two. **No gap. No comment. No "let me think".**
3. Continue through all ten.

Save the whole thing as `day-12-speed-round.m4a`.

**The rule: no restarts and no skipping.** If a question defeats you, spend the thirty seconds saying why it is difficult. That still counts as answering.

**Review (4 min).** Play it back once. Mark which questions were your best three and which were your worst three.

Almost everyone finds the second half easier than the first — not because those questions were easier, but because by then you had given up composing.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** the round runs alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** have them ask the questions and keep time. A human asking is faster and less forgiving than reading them yourself.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 12 — Speed Round Interviewer** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **questions 8, 9 and 10** only. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Flow** | Did the late answers move more freely than the early ones? |
| **Hesitation** | How much searching remained by question ten? |
| **Clarity** | Did thirty seconds produce a real answer? |

Scoring the last three rather than the first three is deliberate. That is where today's learning actually landed.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log which was harder: the first three questions or the last three. Nearly everyone says the first. Write down why you think that is.

## Stretch (optional)

Run ten more with `hard` prompts, at twenty seconds each. Abstract questions with less time is the exact shape of a difficult meeting.

## Tomorrow
