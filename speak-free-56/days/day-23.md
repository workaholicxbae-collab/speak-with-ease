# Day 23 — Childhood, 2 Minutes

**Week 4 · Storytelling & Personality · ~21 minutes · Format: personal narrative**

## Why today matters

Yesterday's spine worked on a recent event you could still see clearly. Today you use it on something older and softer — a childhood memory, where the details are fuzzy and you have to reconstruct rather than report.

This story comes back on Day 26 and again on Day 28. Choose one you do not mind living with.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say aloud the name of the street you grew up on, and one thing you could see from the window.
2. Describe one smell from your childhood. Ten seconds.
3. Say aloud: *"I am allowed to tell this badly the first time."*

## The concept (2 min)

Old memories are stored as fragments — an image, a feeling, a single line someone said. They are not stored as narratives.

So telling one is not recall. It is *construction*: you take four or five fragments and build a sequence that holds them.

This is why childhood stories are harder than yesterday's commute, and why they are worth practising. Most of what you will ever want to tell someone about yourself is built this way.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Choose (2 min).** One childhood memory with something that changed in it — a day, an incident, a discovery. Not a general period. A specific occasion.

**Fragments first (2 min).** Say aloud, in any order, five things you remember: a place, a person, a sound, something said, how it ended. Do not organise them yet.

**Round 1 — Rough (3 min).** Put the fragments through yesterday's spine. Two minutes, recorded, one take. It will be untidy. Save as `day-23-childhood-1.m4a`.

**Round 2 — Ordered (3 min).** Same memory. This time fix only the order — nothing else. Two minutes. Save as `day-23-childhood-2.m4a`.

**The rule: no new facts in Round 2.** Same content, better sequence. This isolates structure from detail, so you can hear what ordering alone is worth.

**Round 3 — For a listener (3 min).** Tell it once more as though to a friend who has never met your family. That means naming people and places instead of assuming them. Save as `day-23-childhood-3.m4a`. **Keep this one — Day 26 uses it.**

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** all three rounds run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** tell them Round 3 and ask which part they could picture most clearly. Their answer tells you where your detail already works.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 23 — Memory Listener** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 3** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Could someone who knows nothing about you follow it? |
| **Expression** | Did you sound like it mattered to you? |
| **Flow** | Did the two minutes hold together? |

Expression carries this one. A childhood story delivered in a report voice is the clearest sign that fear is still flattening you.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also note the filename of Round 3. You need it on Day 26.

## Stretch (optional)

Tell the same memory from another person's point of view — your parent, your sibling, the other child. Same events, different narrator.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow you control the clock. The same story in thirty seconds, ninety seconds and three minutes — by choice, not by accident.
