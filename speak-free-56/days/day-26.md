# Day 26 — Sensory Pass

**Week 4 · Storytelling & Personality · ~21 minutes · Format: sensory upgrade pass**

## Why today matters

Your stories are accurate and grey. They report what happened without letting anyone see it — because under pressure you reach for the safest word, and safe words are always vague.

Today you go back to your Day 23 childhood story and add what makes people picture it. Not more words. Better ones.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say "it was a nice day". Now say it again using weather, light and temperature. No "nice".
2. Say "the food was good". Again with smell, texture and heat. No "good".
3. Say aloud: *"Vague is safe. Specific is interesting."*

## The concept (2 min)

[Five senses](../resources/video-library.md#storytelling--days-22-23-25-26), and you use one.

Almost every learner describes only what things **looked** like. Sound, smell, texture and taste are sitting unused — and smell in particular is the one that makes a listener feel they were there.

The upgrade is a swap, not an addition:

- *a big room* → *a room with a ceiling too high to heat*
- *it was noisy* → *you had to lean in to be heard*
- *old house* → *the smell of damp carpet*

Same length. Completely different picture.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Re-listen (2 min).** Play `day-23-childhood-3.m4a`. Write down every vague word you hear: *nice, big, old, good, bad, strange, interesting*.

**Upgrade the list (3 min).** For each vague word, say aloud a specific replacement. Do not write these down — say them. You need them available at speaking speed, not reading speed.

**Round 1 — Sensory retell (4 min).** Tell the same childhood story, two minutes, **replacing every vague word** with something concrete. Save as `day-26-sensory-1.m4a`.

**The rule: swap, do not add.** The story must stay the same length. If it grows to three minutes you are padding, not upgrading.

**Round 2 — One sense each beat (4 min).** Tell it again. In each spine beat, use a different sense — sound in one, smell in another, texture in a third. Two minutes. Save as `day-26-sensory-2.m4a`.

Forcing one sense per beat prevents you defaulting to sight for the whole story.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** both rounds run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** tell them Round 2, then ask what they can picture. If they describe something you never explicitly said, the detail did its job.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 26 — Vague Word Hunter** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 2** once, then to `day-23-childhood-3.m4a`. Score **Round 2** on these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Expression** | Did the detail bring the telling alive? |
| **Clarity** | Did specifics sharpen the story or clutter it? |
| **Flow** | Did hunting for better words slow you down? |

Flow is the one at risk. Reaching for a precise word costs time at first. If Flow dropped, that is expected — it recovers within a week.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log how many vague words you found in the Day 23 recording. That count is a useful thing to know about your own defaults.

## Stretch (optional)

Retell it with **no visual detail at all** — sound, smell, touch and taste only. Difficult, and it permanently breaks the habit of describing only what things looked like.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow you tell it to an actual face. Not a recorder, not an AI — a person whose expression you have to read while you speak.
