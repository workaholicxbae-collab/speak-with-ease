# Day 39 — Explain It Simply

**Week 6 · Professional English · ~21 minutes · Format: teach-back**

## Why today matters

You are fluent about your own work — until you notice that much of that fluency is jargon. The technical words are doing the work, and they are the same in every language, so they were never really English.

Take them away and you find out how much you can actually explain.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say what your company does in one sentence, no industry words.
2. Say what you personally do, no job title, no acronyms.
3. Say aloud: *"If they can't follow it, I haven't explained it."*

## The concept (2 min)

Three tools replace every technical term:

**Analogy** — *"It's like a filing system, but it decides where things go on its own."*
**Purpose** — say what it is *for* before what it *is*. *"It stops us sending the same email twice."*
**Consequence** — say what breaks without it. *"If nobody did this, customers would get charged twice."*

Purpose-first is the strongest of the three, and the one almost nobody uses. People explain mechanism and expect the listener to work out why it matters.

## Today's work (13 min)

**List your jargon (2 min).** Say your job out loud for 30 seconds. Every technical word you use, write it down. Most people produce six to twelve.

**Round 1 — Ninety seconds, no jargon (4 min).** Explain your job to a twelve-year-old. Ninety seconds. **None** of your listed words. Save as `day-39-simple-1.m4a`.

**The rule: if a banned word slips out, stop and restate that sentence.** This is the only day with a stop-and-restate rule. Today accuracy of explanation matters more than momentum.

**Round 2 — One analogy (4 min).** Same explanation, built around **one** analogy that runs the whole way through. Ninety seconds. Save as `day-39-simple-2.m4a`.

A single sustained analogy is far stronger than three unrelated ones.

**Round 3 — Thirty seconds (3 min).** Same explanation, compressed to thirty seconds, still no jargon. Save as `day-39-simple-3.m4a`.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** all three rounds run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** explain it to someone outside your field and ask them to explain it back. Whatever they get wrong is what you explained badly.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 39 — Twelve-Year-Old** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 2** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Could a twelve-year-old repeat the main idea? |
| **Flow** | Did avoiding jargon slow you badly? |
| **Expression** | Did you sound like you were teaching or reciting? |

Flow usually drops today. Jargon is a shortcut, and removing it costs speed until the plain-English versions become automatic.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log how many jargon words you listed, and how many escaped in Round 1.

## Stretch (optional)

Explain the most technical part of your work — the part you would normally say is "too complicated to explain" — in sixty seconds. That phrase is almost always cover for not having tried.
