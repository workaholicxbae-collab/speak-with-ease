# Day 40 — The 60-Second Update

**Week 6 · Professional English · ~20 minutes · Format: timed update**

## Why today matters

"Quick update from you?" It is the most frequent speaking task in working life, and the one where nervous speakers waste the most words.

Sixty seconds. Three parts. Nothing else. Today you make it a reflex so it stops costing you thought.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say what you did yesterday at work in three sentences. Stop at three.
2. Say what you are doing today in two sentences.
3. Say aloud: *"Done, doing, blocked. That's the whole thing."*

## The concept (2 min)

The structure is three parts, in this order:

> **Done** — what has actually finished since last time. Not "worked on" — finished.
> **Doing** — what is in progress and when it lands.
> **Blocked** — what you need from someone in the room, named specifically.

That is it. No preamble, no apology, no "so, um, yeah, basically".

Most people ramble in updates because they start talking before deciding which of the three they are in. Naming the part silently as you move through it is what keeps the sixty seconds tight.

The Blocked section is the one people skip, and it is the only part that requires the meeting to exist. If nothing is blocked, say "nothing blocked" — do not fill it.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Something to listen to:** [video library](../resources/video-library.md#meetings-and-professional-english--days-36-39-40-41) — take the first clip that fits. Do not browse.

**Round 1 — Cold (3 min).** Give a 60-second update on your actual current work. No preparation, no notes. Save as `day-40-cold.m4a`.

**Listen (2 min).** Count how many seconds passed before you said anything concrete. Most people burn fifteen on warm-up phrases.

**Round 2 — Structured (3 min).** Same update, using Done / Doing / Blocked. Sixty seconds. Save as `day-40-structured.m4a`.

**The rules:** first sentence must be Done · no "so", "um", "basically" or "just" at the start · stop at sixty seconds even mid-sentence.

**Round 3 — Thirty seconds (3 min).** Same content, half the time. Save as `day-40-thirty.m4a`.

**Round 4 — Bad news (2 min).** Sixty seconds where the main item is something that went wrong. Same structure. Say it plainly and do not bury it. Save as `day-40-badnews.m4a`.

Delivering bad news clearly is a senior skill, and burying it is the most common way people damage their credibility.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** all four rounds run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** give them the update and cut yourself off hard at sixty seconds. Ask what they think is blocked.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 40 — Standup Chair** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 4** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Were Done, Doing and Blocked all distinguishable? |
| **Flow** | Any wasted words at the start? |
| **Expression** | Did the bad news sound steady, or defensive? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log your Round 1 warm-up time in seconds — how long before the first concrete word. That number should be near zero by Day 56.

## Stretch (optional)

Give the update to an audience who does not know your project at all. You have to supply context without spending your sixty seconds on it.
