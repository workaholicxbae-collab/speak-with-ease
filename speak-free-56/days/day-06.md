# Day 6 — Three Moods

**Week 1 · Break the Silence · ~21 minutes · Format: read-aloud with emotion shift**

## Why today matters

In your first language you are funny, sharp, warm, sarcastic. In English you are flat — not because your personality disappears, but because fear compresses the voice into one careful monotone.

Today you get the range back. And because the words are already written, you have nothing to compose and nothing to fear. All your attention goes into *how* it sounds.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Hum from your lowest comfortable note to your highest. Three times.
2. Say "oh really" four ways: bored · shocked · delighted · suspicious.
3. Laugh out loud on purpose for five seconds. It will feel ridiculous. It also unlocks the throat better than any exercise here.

## The concept (2 min)

Meaning does not live only in words. It lives in pace, volume, pitch and pause.

Say *"that's great"* slowly and flat and it means the opposite. Say it fast and high and it is genuine. Identical words, opposite messages — and every listener reads the delivery, not the dictionary.

You already do this perfectly in your first language, without thinking. Today you find out the skill transfers.

## Today's work (14 min)

**Pick your paragraph (2 min).** Any 4–5 sentences of ordinary English. A news paragraph, a page from a book, even the "Why today matters" section above. Content does not matter — neutral is better.

**Read 1 — Bored (3 min).** Record it as though you have said it a thousand times and want to leave. Flat, low, slow. Save as `day-06-bored.m4a`.

**Read 2 — Excited (3 min).** Same paragraph. You cannot wait to tell someone. Faster, higher, push the key words. Save as `day-06-excited.m4a`.

**Read 3 — Angry (3 min).** Same paragraph. Controlled anger, not shouting. Slower, harder consonants, heavy stress. Save as `day-06-angry.m4a`.

**The rule: exaggerate past the point of comfort.** What feels theatrical from the inside sounds merely *normal* on playback. Every learner under-does this on the first attempt.

**Listen back (3 min).** Play all three in order. If they sound like the same recording three times, do Read 2 again and push twice as hard.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** the three reads above run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** read one version aloud without saying which mood it is. Ask them to guess. If they cannot, you were not exaggerating enough.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 6 — Mood Guesser** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to the **excited** version once. Score these three:

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Expression** | Was there genuine variation, or polite variation? |
| **Clarity** | Did the emotion help the meaning or bury it? |
| **Flow** | Did it move at the pace the mood demanded? |

See [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md).

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Hesitation is usually low today — the words were given to you. Note that, because it isolates the point: when composing is removed, you are fluent. Composing is the bottleneck, not English.

## Stretch (optional)

Fourth read: **kind.** As if explaining something to a nervous friend. Slow, warm, softer consonants. This is the most useful register of the four and the hardest to fake.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow is lighter. Twelve minutes. You open the Day 1 recording for the first time — and almost nobody hears what they expected to hear.
