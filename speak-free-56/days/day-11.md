# Day 11 — Live Commentary

**Week 2 · Think in English · ~22 minutes · Format: silent-video narration**

## Why today matters

Every task so far let you set the pace. You could pause, gather yourself, continue.

Today something else sets the pace. A video plays, and it does not wait. There is no room to translate — not because you decided to stop, but because there is physically no time.

This is the hardest day of Week 2 and the one that proves the point.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Narrate your own hands for thirty seconds, live, as you move them.
2. Look out of a window and describe what is happening right now, for thirty seconds.
3. Say aloud: *"I describe what I see. I do not wait for a good sentence."*

## The concept (2 min)

Commentary is speaking with the composing step removed. You are not deciding what to talk about — the screen decides. Your only job is to put words on what is already there.

Sports commentators are not more fluent than other people. They have simply practised the one skill of speaking at the speed of events, and it turns out that skill is trainable in about twenty minutes a day.

## Today's work (14 min)

**Find a video (2 min).** Three minutes long, with people doing things — cooking, a street scene, a match, a repair. **Mute it.** Subtitles off.

Nothing with fast cuts. A slow, continuous scene is far better for a first attempt.

**Pass 1 — Watch (3 min).** Play it muted. Say nothing. Notice how much is happening.

**Pass 2 — Narrate (3 min).** Play again. Describe what is happening as it happens. Out loud, no recording yet.

You will fall behind. When you do, **do not catch up** — drop what you missed and describe what is on screen now. Falling behind and rejoining is the skill.

**Pass 3 — Record (3 min).** Play once more, narrating at full voice, recording yourself. Save as `day-11-commentary.m4a`.

**The rule: never describe the past.** If it has left the screen, it is gone. Present tense only.

**Review (3 min).** Play your recording against the muted video. Where you went silent, the video was moving faster than your English. Note those moments.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** the three passes run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** have them watch the screen while you narrate from a position where you can both see it. Ask afterwards whether they could have followed the action from your words alone.

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Flow** | Did you keep pace, or stall and restart? |
| **Hesitation** | How much dead air was there? |
| **Recovery** | After falling behind, did you rejoin cleanly? |

Do not expect good scores. A 2 or 3 here is normal and means the difficulty was set correctly.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

If today felt impossible, note that too. Re-run it tomorrow with a slower video before moving to Day 12 — this one is worth getting.

## Stretch (optional)

Narrate a video with fast cuts, or one where people are talking so you must describe their expressions and reactions rather than just their actions.
