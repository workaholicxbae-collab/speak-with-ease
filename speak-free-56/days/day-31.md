# Day 31 — Steelman

**Week 5 · Opinions & Disagreement · ~21 minutes · Format: steelman**

## Why today matters

Arguing a side you do not hold removes the last excuse. You cannot fall back on sincerity, so all that is left is construction — and construction is the skill.

It also makes you far harder to argue with. Someone who can state your position better than you can is not easy to dismiss.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say aloud one thing you firmly believe.
2. Now say the strongest sentence someone who disagrees would use against it.
3. Say aloud: *"I can hold an idea without agreeing with it."*

## The concept (2 min)

A **strawman** is the weak version of the other side — the version that is easy to knock down and that nobody actually holds.

A **steelman** is the strongest, most reasonable version. The one a thoughtful person on that side would recognise as fair.

Steelmanning is not politeness. It is precision. And in English, it is unusually good practice because you cannot lean on feeling — you have to build the argument deliberately, sentence by sentence, which is exactly the muscle Week 5 is training.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Something to listen to:** [video library](../resources/video-library.md#opinions-and-disagreement--days-2934) — take the first clip that fits. Do not browse.

**Choose (2 min).** One position you genuinely disagree with. From [../resources/prompt-bank.md](../resources/prompt-bank.md), any `hard` prompt works. Real disagreement matters — a topic you are neutral on teaches nothing.

**Round 1 — The weak version (3 min).** Deliberately argue the other side *badly* for 60 seconds. Make it thin and easy to dismiss.

Doing this first is useful. It shows you what your brain reaches for by default when it does not want to be persuaded.

**Round 2 — The strong version (5 min).** Now argue that same side as well as you possibly can, for two minutes.

Give the best reason, an example, and acknowledge what it costs. Save as `day-31-steelman.m4a`.

**The rule: no sabotage.** No "but obviously that's wrong". No tone that signals you disagree. If a listener could tell which side you are really on, you have not steelmanned it.

**Round 3 — Your own view, after (3 min).** Now argue your actual position for 90 seconds. Save as `day-31-own-view.m4a`.

Notice whether it changed. It usually gets narrower and more precise, because you now know what it has to survive.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** all three rounds run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** find someone who actually holds the view and ask whether your version was fair. Their answer is the only real grade.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 31 — Steelman Judge** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 2** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Was the argument actually followable? |
| **Expression** | Did you sound sincere, or leak your real view? |
| **Flow** | Did building an unfamiliar argument slow you down? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log whether Round 3 came out differently from what you would have said before Round 2.

## Stretch (optional)

Steelman a position you find genuinely objectionable. Much harder, and the clearest possible proof that you can separate speaking from believing.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow you disagree with a person rather than an idea — and do it without apologising first.
