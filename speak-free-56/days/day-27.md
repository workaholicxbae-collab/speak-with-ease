# Day 27 — Live Swap

**Week 4 · Storytelling & Personality · ~20 minutes · Format: live delivery to a human**

## Why today matters

A recorder never looks confused. It never glances at its phone, never laughs in the wrong place, never waits for you to get to the point.

A person does all of those, and you have to keep talking anyway. Today you tell your story to a face and read the response while it is happening.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say your story's first line aloud three times, looking at yourself or straight ahead.
2. Say your last line three times. Underplayed, as on Day 25.
3. Say aloud: *"If they look confused, I explain. I don't stop."*

## The concept (2 min)

Watching a listener is a second job running alongside speaking, and it is the one that makes live telling harder than recording.

There are three signals and three responses:

- **They look confused** → back up one step and say it differently. Do not restart.
- **They look bored** → skip to the turn. Cut the setup.
- **They lean in** → slow down. You have them; do not rush the ending.

You have been reading faces your whole life. The only new part is doing it while producing English, and that is what today builds.

## Today's work (12 min)

**Choose (1 min).** Your best story from this week — Day 23, 25 or 26. Use the ninety-second length from Day 24.

**Rehearse once (3 min).** Out loud, alone, one pass. Do not memorise it. You are warming the shape, not fixing the words.

**Tell it (5 min).** To one real person. Face to face if possible, video call if not.

**The rules:** no notes · watch their face · adjust rather than restart · stop when the ending lands.

**Debrief (3 min).** Within five minutes, record 90 seconds: where they reacted, where they drifted, what you changed mid-telling. Save as `day-27-debrief.m4a`.

That third question is the important one. Adjusting mid-story is a genuine skill and you will not notice you did it unless you look.

## Interactive layer

**With a person (default today):** the telling above.

**Solo fallback — genuinely nobody available:** tell it to a video camera with the front-facing view on, so you watch your own face while speaking. Weaker, and you should know it is weaker. Then do the real version within two days.

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to your debrief once. Score **the live telling** — not the debrief — on these three, see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Expression** | Did you tell it, or recite it? |
| **Recovery** | When they looked lost, did you adjust or freeze? |
| **Flow** | Did watching them interrupt your speaking? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log one line: **where did they react?** Real reactions are the most reliable feedback in this program — more honest than any score you give yourself.

## Stretch (optional)

Tell the same story to a second person immediately afterwards. The second telling is always better, and noticing why teaches you more than the first one did.
