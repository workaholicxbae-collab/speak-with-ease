# Day 47 — Deliberate Mistakes

**Week 7 · Pressure & Spontaneity · ~21 minutes · Format: desensitisation drill**

## Why today matters

You have spent your whole English life avoiding mistakes. Avoidance is what makes them so expensive when they happen — the alarm goes off, you stop, and the stopping is what people actually notice.

Today you make mistakes on purpose, repeatedly, until the alarm stops firing. This is the most important day of Week 7 and the one people are most tempted to skip.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say a sentence with a deliberate grammar error. Out loud. Do not correct it.
2. Say two more. Leave them all standing.
3. Say aloud: *"A mistake I speak past costs nothing."*

## The concept (2 min)

Fear of mistakes is a conditioned alarm, and alarms fade through exposure — not through avoidance.

Every time you make an error and *stop*, you confirm the alarm was right: something bad happened and action was required. Every time you make an error and *keep going*, you teach the opposite — that nothing followed, and the sentence carried on regardless.

The goal is not to make mistakes forever. It is to make the alarm quiet enough that accuracy becomes something you choose, rather than something fear demands.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Round 1 — Wrong tenses (4 min).** Speak for two minutes on any `easy` prompt, deliberately using the wrong tense at least ten times. *"Yesterday I go to the shop and I am buying bread."*

**Never correct yourself.** Record. Save as `day-47-tenses.m4a`.

**Round 2 — Wrong words (4 min).** Two minutes on a new prompt, deliberately using approximately-wrong words. *"I sat on the table and ate my chair."* Keep going as if correct. Save as `day-47-words.m4a`.

**Round 3 — Natural speech, no self-correction (4 min).** Two minutes on a `medium` prompt, speaking normally. **You may not correct yourself even once**, whatever happens. Save as `day-47-nocorrect.m4a`.

**The rule for all three: no "sorry", no "I mean", no restarting.** Errors stand.

**Notice (1 min).** In Round 3, count the times you *wanted* to correct yourself. That number is the size of the habit.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** all three rounds run alone.

**Upgrade — with a person:** ask them to listen without reacting to any error. Then ask afterwards how many they noticed. It is always fewer than you think.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 47 — Unflinching Listener** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 3** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Flow** | Did banning self-correction make you faster? |
| **Recovery** | After an error, did you continue cleanly? |
| **Clarity** | Did the mistakes actually damage understanding? |

That last one is the finding. Play it back and ask honestly whether a listener would have been confused. Almost always, the answer is no — the errors were audible to you and invisible to everyone else.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log how many times you wanted to self-correct in Round 3, and how many errors actually harmed the meaning. The gap between those two numbers is what this day is for.

## Stretch (optional)

Have a real conversation today with no self-correction at all. Not deliberate errors — just no going back. Notice whether anybody reacts.
