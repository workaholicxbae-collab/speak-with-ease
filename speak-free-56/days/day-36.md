# Day 36 — Meeting Moves

**Week 6 · Professional English · ~21 minutes · Format: meeting-phrase drill**

## Why today matters

You sit in meetings with something worth saying and no way in. The conversation never pauses long enough, and by the time it does, the moment is gone.

You have been waiting for a gap. Gaps do not appear — you make one, and there is a polite phrase for doing it.

## Warm-up (3 min)

1. Say aloud: *"Can I jump in there?"* Five times, at normal volume.
2. Say: *"Sorry, just to check —"* five times.
3. Say aloud: *"Waiting for a gap means never speaking."*

## The concept (2 min)

Four moves cover almost everything you need in a meeting. Each is a fixed phrase, not a sentence you compose.

**Interrupt** — *"Can I jump in there?"* · *"Just on that point —"*
**Clarify** — *"Sorry, just to check — do you mean X?"* · *"Can you say more about…?"*
**Summarise** — *"So are we saying we'll do X?"* · *"To pull that together —"*
**Park** — *"Can we come back to that?"* · *"That might be a separate conversation."*

Summarising is the most undervalued. It requires no opinion and no new information, it makes you sound like the most organised person present, and it costs about eight words.

## Today's work (13 min)

**Something to listen to:** [video library](../resources/video-library.md#meetings-and-professional-english--days-36-39-40-41) — take the first clip that fits. Do not browse.

**Drill (5 min).** Say each of the eight phrases **five times aloud**. Forty repetitions.

Vary the delivery — urgent, relaxed, quiet. In a real meeting you will need the relaxed version, and it is the hardest to produce under pressure.

**Round 1 — Deploy (5 min).** Play a recording of people talking — a podcast, a panel, any discussion. Record yourself.

Every time you hear something you could respond to, **use one of the four moves out loud**. Aim for at least eight uses in five minutes.

Save as `day-36-moves.m4a`.

**The rule: never wait for a pause.** Speak over the recording. That is the point — you are training the act of entering, not the act of waiting.

**Round 2 — Summarise (3 min).** Play two minutes of the same discussion. Then summarise what was said in three sentences, recorded. Save as `day-36-summary.m4a`.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** both rounds run alone against a recording.

**Upgrade — with a person:** ask them to talk for three minutes about anything and interrupt them politely four times.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 36 — Meeting Floor** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (3 min)

Listen to **Round 2** once. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Did the summary capture the point in three sentences? |
| **Flow** | Did the phrases arrive without a search? |
| **Expression** | Did you sound like someone entitled to the floor? |

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md).

Also log how many times you entered in Round 1. Under five means you were still waiting for permission.

## Stretch (optional)

Summarise a real meeting out loud afterwards, in three sentences, alone. If you cannot, you were not following it as closely as you thought.

## Tomorrow

Tomorrow you introduce yourself three ways — ten seconds, thirty, ninety — because the wrong length is why introductions feel awkward.
