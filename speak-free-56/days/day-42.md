# Day 42 — Mock Interview

**Week 6 · Professional English · ~12 minutes · Format: recorded interview and reflection**

## Why today matters

Six days of professional drills. Today they run together, under time, unedited.

Five questions, no restarts, one file. This is the closest thing to a real professional test in the program, and it is deliberately short.

## Warm-up (2 min)

No vocal warm-up. Instead:

Write one line in [../trackers/progress-tracker.md](../trackers/progress-tracker.md): **which of the five questions below you least want to be asked.**

Name it before you start. It is usually the one you handle worst, and knowing that in advance is worth more than avoiding it.

## The concept (2 min)

Interviews are not tests of fluency. They are tests of whether your answers arrive in a shape someone can use.

You now have every shape you need: the introduction from Day 37, STAR from Day 38, plain explanation from Day 39, and the honest gap from Day 41.

Today is not about learning anything new. It is about reaching for the right tool while under mild pressure — which is a different skill from having the tool.

## Today's work (6 min)

**Set up (1 min).** Record continuously through all five questions. **No stopping between them.** Read each question aloud, then answer.

**The five questions (5 min).** Roughly 60 seconds each:

1. *Tell me about yourself.* — the ninety-second version from Day 37
2. *Tell me about a difficult problem you solved.* — STAR
3. *Explain what your team does to someone outside the industry.* — no jargon
4. *What's a mistake you've made, and what did you do about it?* — STAR, and do not soften it
5. *What don't you know yet that you'd need to learn for this role?* — the honest gap

Save as `day-42-interview.m4a`.

**The rules:** one take · no restarts · no editing · if you stumble, keep going.

## Interactive layer

**Solo fallback (default):** read the questions yourself and answer.

**Upgrade — with a person:** have them ask the questions and add one unscripted follow-up to any answer. The unplanned follow-up is the realistic part.

**Upgrade — with AI:** use the **Day 42 — Mock Interviewer** prompt in [../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md](../resources/ai-roleplay-prompts.md).

## Record & review (2 min)

Listen once, all the way through. Score these three — see [../resources/rubric.md](../resources/rubric.md):

| Dimension | Score 1–5 |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Did each answer have a recognisable shape? |
| **Recovery** | After the weakest moment, did you rebuild? |
| **Flow** | Did five answers hold without collapse? |

**Average the week (1 min).** Add Days 36–42, divide by seven, log as **Week 6 average**.

Compare with Week 5. Most people improve here — professional content is familiar, and structure does a lot of the work that fluency was doing before.

## Confidence checkpoint

**Rate 1–10: how much did you hesitate today?** Log it.

Then check the question you named in the warm-up. Was it actually the worst one? Often it is not — the anticipated difficulty and the real one rarely match.

## Stretch (optional)

Answer all five again immediately, one take. The second run is always sharper, and the gap tells you how much of today was nerves rather than ability.

## Tomorrow

Week 7, and the preparation stops. From tomorrow you get fifteen seconds or less before you have to speak — and recovery becomes the only thing that matters.
