# The Rubric — Five Dimensions

Five things. Scored 1 to 5. You use **exactly three on any given day** — never all five.

That limit is deliberate. Score everything and you notice nothing; you produce a vague sense of "that was bad" and learn no more than you knew before. Score three and you get an actual signal.

Each day tells you which three. Do not substitute your own.

**Notice what is missing from this list: grammar and accent.** Neither is scored, on any day, ever. Not because they do not exist, but because watching them is what taught you to stop mid-sentence.

---

## 1. Flow
*Did the speech move?*

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Stopped repeatedly. More silence than speech. |
| **2** | Started and stalled. Several full stops mid-thought. |
| **3** | Moved, with visible effort. One or two real stalls. |
| **4** | Mostly continuous. Pauses land between ideas, not inside them. |
| **5** | Continuous throughout. Any pause sounded chosen. |

Flow is not speed. A slow, unbroken speaker scores 5. A fast speaker who stops four times scores 2.

---

## 2. Hesitation
*How much did you search?*

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Searching constantly. Filler in almost every sentence. |
| **2** | Frequent hunting for words. Heavy filler. |
| **3** | Noticeable searching, but you kept going. |
| **4** | Occasional hesitation, quickly resolved. |
| **5** | Rare. You reached for a word and moved on. |

The enemy here is the *search*, not the pause. A two-second silence while you think is fine. Two seconds of "ummmm" is the habit being trained out.

---

## 3. Clarity
*Would a stranger have understood?*

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Hard to follow. Ideas tangled or unfinished. |
| **2** | Followable in places. Lost the thread more than once. |
| **3** | Understandable. Some sentences went in circles. |
| **4** | Clear. Ideas arrived in a sensible order. |
| **5** | Immediately clear. A stranger would get it first time. |

Clarity is about structure, not correctness. "Me and him goes to the shop yesterday" is perfectly clear. Score it 5.

---

## 4. Expression
*Did you sound like a person?*

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Flat. Robotic. One note throughout. |
| **2** | Mostly flat. Occasional life. |
| **3** | Some variation in pace and pitch. |
| **4** | Varied and natural. Emphasis where it belonged. |
| **5** | Genuinely engaging. You could hear what you thought about it. |

Most learners at this stage score 1–2 without realising. Fear flattens the voice. This is the dimension that moves fastest once you stop being afraid.

---

## 5. Recovery
*What happened after it went wrong?*

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Went wrong, stopped dead, restarted from the top. |
| **2** | Long freeze, then recovered with difficulty. |
| **3** | Noticeable stumble, then carried on. |
| **4** | Rephrased smoothly and kept moving. |
| **5** | Recovered so cleanly a listener would not have noticed. |

If nothing went wrong, do not score this dimension — write `n/a`. A 5 for a session where nothing was tested is a lie to yourself.

Recovery is the single best predictor of who still sounds confident at Day 56.

---

## How to score honestly

**Score immediately after listening once.** Not twice. Your first impression is the accurate one; the second listen is where you start negotiating.

**Do not compare to a native speaker.** Compare to your own last recording. That is the only comparison in this program that means anything.

**Expect scores to drop in Weeks 5 and 7.** The work gets harder. A 3 in Week 7 is worth more than a 4 in Week 2. If your numbers never dip, the days are too easy and you should take the Stretch option.
