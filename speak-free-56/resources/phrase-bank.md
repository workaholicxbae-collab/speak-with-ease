# Phrase Bank

Grouped by **function**, not topic — because you do not need "restaurant phrases", you need something to say when you have lost your sentence.

**How to use this:** do not read it. Pick one group, say every phrase in it aloud five times, and use two of them today. A phrase you have read is not available under pressure. A phrase you have said forty times is.

---

## 1. Opening

Getting in, starting, claiming the floor.

- How's your day going?
- Busy week?
- Have you been here before?
- What have you got planned this weekend?
- Sorry, do you know if…?
- Can I jump in there?
- Just on that point —
- Can I ask you something?
- I've been meaning to ask —
- Quick question —
- Do you have two minutes?
- Something I wanted to raise —

---

## 2. Buying time

You need three seconds. These give you three seconds without sounding lost.

- That's a fair question.
- Let me think about that for a second.
- Two things on that.
- Good question — give me a moment.
- The short answer is… and then there's a longer one.
- I want to get this right, so —
- How do I put this —
- It's worth separating two things here.
- Let me come at that differently.
- Before I answer that —

**"Two things on that"** is the strongest phrase in this bank. It buys you time *and* generates structure — once you have said "two things", you will find two things, because the sentence has committed you.

---

## 3. Clarifying

Making sure you understood, and making sure they did.

- Sorry, just to check — do you mean X?
- Can you say more about that?
- When you say X, do you mean…?
- I want to make sure I've got this right.
- So if I understand you correctly —
- Sorry, I missed that last part.
- Could you say that again more slowly?
- Just so we're on the same page —
- Is that the same as…?
- What do you mean exactly?

Asking someone to repeat themselves is not a confession of weakness. Confident speakers do it constantly, because they would rather ask than guess.

---

## 4. Disagreeing

Say the disagreement first. Soften after, if you want to.

- I see what you mean, but —
- I'm not sure I agree with that.
- I'd push back on that slightly.
- That's not how I'd read it.
- I think there's another way to look at this.
- I understand that view, but I don't share it.
- Could we also consider…?
- I don't think that's the main problem, though.
- Where I'd differ is —
- I'd argue the opposite, actually.

Notice that not one of these starts with "sorry". That is deliberate. Apology belongs to things you did wrong, and having a view is not one of them.

---

## 5. Softening

For when you genuinely want to reduce the force — not as a reflex before every sentence.

- I might be wrong, but —
- From what I've seen —
- In my experience —
- I tend to think —
- My instinct is —
- It seems to me —
- Correct me if I'm off here —
- This is just my read on it.

**Use one, never three.** Stacked softeners — *"sorry, I might be wrong, but I just think maybe possibly…"* — delete the sentence they were attached to.

---

## 6. Recovering

You have lost the sentence, the word, or the thread. These get you out.

- Let me rephrase that.
- What I mean is —
- Sorry, let me start that sentence again.
- The word's gone — it's the thing you use to…
- Coming back to what I was saying —
- Let me just finish this point.
- Where was I? Right —
- Anyway, the point is —
- I've lost my thread — give me a second.
- Bear with me.

"I've lost my thread" said calmly is completely normal English. Said in panic it sounds like a crisis. The words are identical; only the delivery differs.

---

## 7. Closing

Ending a turn, a call, or a conversation cleanly.

- So that's where we are.
- That's really all I wanted to say.
- Does that answer it?
- Shall we leave it there?
- I'll send that over by Thursday.
- Let's pick this up next week.
- Anyway, I should let you go.
- Good to talk — see you soon.
- So are we saying we'll do X?
- To pull that together —

**"So are we saying we'll do X?"** is the most valuable sentence in any meeting. It needs no opinion, no new information, and it makes you the person who brought the discussion to a decision.

---

## Drilling these

Take **one group per week**. Say every phrase five times aloud on the first day, then use at least two of them in real speech that week.

Do not attempt the whole bank. Twelve phrases you can produce without thinking are worth more than a hundred you have read once.

Suggested order, roughly matching the program:

| Week | Group |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Opening, Recovering |
| 3 | Clarifying, Closing |
| 5 | Disagreeing, Softening |
| 6–7 | Buying time |
